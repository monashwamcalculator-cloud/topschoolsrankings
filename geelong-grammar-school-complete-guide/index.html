History & Heritage of Geelong Grammar School

Foundation and Early Years (1855-1914)

Geelong Grammar School opened on 1 October 1855 as a private venture under the Church of England's auspices. The school began with just 14 pupils in two semi-detached houses in Villamanta Street, Geelong, Victoria.

Five clergymen, including first headmaster George Vance, founded the school to provide quality secondary education for sons of Geelong's growing middle class. By 1858, the school moved into grand neo-Gothic buildings in central Geelong.

The school gained recognition as "Australia's Eton" by 1907, even adopting the same shade of blue (Eton blue) for its school colour. During this period, students came from all around Australia, particularly Victoria's Western District.

Relocation to Corio Campus (1914)

By 1911, the school had outgrown its inner-city location. Inspired by English public schools like Eton, the Council sought open space with playing fields to promote "a healthy mind in a healthy body."

The school settled on land at Corio (north of Geelong city) with beautiful views over Limeburners Bay. Students moved into new red brick buildings in 1914.

Over subsequent decades, the chapel, dining hall, additional boarding houses, and Junior School were completed. Enrolments grew significantly under headmaster Sir James Darling (1930–1961).

Four Unique Campuses Explained

Geelong Grammar School is Australia's largest co-educational boarding school, caring for over 1,600 students across four unique campuses from ELC (Early Learning Centre) to Year 12.

1. Corio Campus (Main Campus)

Location:

50 Biddlecombe Avenue, Corio 3214, Victoria, Australia

Age Range:

ELC to Year 12 Features:

  • Red brick buildings dating back to 1912

  • Overlooks Corio Bay and Limeburners Bay

  • SPACE (School for Performing Arts and Creative Education) - completed 2015

  • Chapel and dining hall

  • Multiple boarding houses for senior boys and girls

  • Junior School (moved from Bostock House in 2026)

The Corio Campus is the heart of the school, offering comprehensive day and boarding options with world-class facilities for performing arts, science, and sports.

2. Toorak Campus

Location:

Toorak, Melbourne, Victoria

Age Range:

ELC to Year 6

History:

Founded in 1887 as Glamorgan Preparatory School by Miss Annie McComas

Key Facts:

  • Acquired by Geelong Grammar in 1947

  • Became co-educational in 1973

  • Renamed Toorak Campus in 2008

  • Former Prime Minister Stanley Melbourne Bruce attended (1891–1895)

Toorak provides exceptional early learning and primary education in Melbourne's prestigious Toorak suburb.

3. Bostock House (Closed 2026)

Historical Note: Bostock House closed at the conclusion of Term 1, 2026. Students and staff relocated to the newly built Junior School at Corio Campus, marking the next chapter in the school's history.

4. Timbertop Campus

Location:

Mt Timbertop, Victorian Alps near Mansfield

Age Range:

Year 9 only

Established:

1953 

Philosophy: Outdoor education inspired by the Outward Bound movement

Timbertop provides an unique "off-grid" wilderness learning experience for Grade 9 students. The campus features:

  • Timber "units" (residential buildings) constructed by students in 1952

  • Chapel of St John the Baptist (opened 1958)

  • Dedicated outdoor education program (established 1955)

  • Focus on developing stamina, courage, adaptability, and persistence

Sir James Darling visioned Timbertop to help students "learn to look after themselves, to find their own occupation, and develop their own capacities."

Conclusion

Geelong Grammar School stands as one of Australia's most prestigious independent schools, combining 165 years of heritage with innovative educational approaches like Positive Education and unique wilderness learning at Timbertop.

With four campuses, over 1,600 students, multiple academic pathways (IB and VCE), and exceptional boarding facilities, Geelong Grammar offers a comprehensive education that prepares students for leading universities worldwide.

While fees range from $19,500 AUD (day school) to $93,840 AUD (full boarding), the school's non-selective enrolment policy, sibling discounts, and world-class facilities make it accessible to families seeking exceptional education.

FAQs

1. What is Geelong Grammar School known for?

Geelong Grammar School is known for being Australia's largest co-educational boarding school with 165+ years of history. It's recognised as "Australia's Eton," pioneering Positive Education, offering IB Diploma and VCE programs, and providing unique wilderness learning at its Timbertop campus for Year 9 students.

2. How much does Geelong Grammar School cost in 2026?

In 2026, Geelong Grammar School fees range from $19,500 AUD to $61,000 AUD for day school (ELC–Year 12), and $89,280 AUD to $93,840 AUD for full boarding (Years 7–12). Primary day school starts around $30,560 AUD, with a 20% sibling discount for 3rd+ children.

3. Is Geelong Grammar School selective?

No, Geelong Grammar School has a non-selective enrolment policy. The school welcomes applications for all year levels at any time from the birth of a child, catering to approximately 1,500 students without entrance test requirements for enrolment.

4. What campuses does Geelong Grammar School have?

Geelong Grammar School operates four campuses: Corio (main campus in Geelong, ELC–Year 12), Toorak (Melbourne, ELC–Year 6), Timbertop (Victorian Alps, Year 9 only), and formerly Bostock House (closed 2026, relocated to Corio).

5. Does Geelong Grammar School offer boarding?

Yes, Geelong Grammar School offers full boarding from Years 5–12 at Corio Campus, with 800 boarders currently. Timbertop provides unique outdoor education boarding for Year 9 only. The school is Australia's largest co-educational boarding school.

6. What academic programs are available at Geelong Grammar?

Geelong Grammar School offers three pathways for Years 11–12: IB Diploma (international recognition), VCE (Victorian Certificate of Education), and VCE with Vocational Focus (VET qualifications). Year 10 prepares students for both IB and VCE programmes.
