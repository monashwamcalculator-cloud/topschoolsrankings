Top 100 Best Public High Schools in America: US Rankings, Admissions & State Guide (2026–27)

The 100 best public high schools in America, ranked for 2026–27 from Niche's 2026 list and checked against U.S. News — admissions and state breakdowns.

Tables, images and URL checked 20 November 2026 · Verify time-sensitive facts

There is no single official list of the best public high schools in America — and the two rankings families trust most do not even agree on the winner. Niche’s 2026 list, released on 29 September 2025, puts the North Carolina School of Science and Mathematics first. U.S. News, in its 2026–27 rankings released on 18 August 2026, puts Central Magnet School in Tennessee first. This guide uses Niche’s 2026 list as its reproducible 100-school spine — the order you see in the table below — and checks the leading schools and the methods against the U.S. News release, so you can see where the two rankings agree and where they part ways.

Every school in the table is a public school: selective magnets, residential academies, charters and neighbourhood comprehensives, but no private schools. The guide covers the full 100-school ranking, which states the schools come from, the five schools at the top, the five different ways students actually get in, and how to choose from the list. This page is a research guide, not an official government endorsement and not a promise of admission or outcomes. A one-place difference is not educationally significant, and the first school on the list is not the right choice for every child.

How We Ranked the Top 100 high schools in the USA

The order uses Niche's 2026 public-high-school methodology and exact published factor weights. Its page displayed badges #1–#8 and then #10 at the time of checking; no #9 entry was present in the page data. To provide exactly 100 schools without inventing a missing school, we preserve every published badge in the last column and number the displayed schools sequentially in the first column. U.S. News is used as a current cross-check, not silently blended into the order.

How Niche turns the factors into a ranking

Niche standardizes each factor’s scores as a z-score, applies a Bayesian adjustment so that schools with more survey responses count as more reliable, applies the weights above and calculates a final score for each school. Schools missing the data for half or more of the factors by weight are excluded from the numerical ranking altogether. Because academics carries 60% of the weight, a school’s state proficiency results, college enrolment and academic survey responses dominate its position — which is why the top of the list is crowded with selective STEM and magnet schools.

What the method does not do

  • It does not sell or reserve ranking positions.
  • It does not treat one exam average as a complete measure of school quality.
  • It does not assume selective and open-admissions schools have comparable intakes.
  • It does not reproduce an outdated table without naming the source edition and check date.
  • It does not replace current admissions, fee, safeguarding or programme verification.

Two rankings, two No. 1s

Niche and U.S. News measure different things, so their winners differ — and only one school, the Davidson Academy, appears in both top fives. Niche weights academics at 60% and leans on surveys and school-reported data; U.S. News weights college readiness at 30% and leans on state assessments and graduation rates. U.S. News scores its schools on six indicators: college readiness (30%), state assessment proficiency (20%), state assessment performance (20%), underserved student performance (10%), college curriculum breadth (10%) and graduation rate (10%), using data from the 2023–24 school year across more than 27,000 public high schools, of which nearly 18,000 were ranked. Central Magnet rose from third place last year to first.

How U.S. News turns its six indicators into a ranking

U.S. News works with RTI International to score each school on the six indicators above, then ranks the schools nationally, by state and by metro area. College readiness — the share of 12th-graders who took and passed AP or IB exams — carries the most weight at 30%, so a school with broad AP or IB participation and strong pass rates has a structural advantage. The two state-assessment indicators together carry 40%, which is why a school in a state with rigorous assessments can rank above a similar school elsewhere. Graduation rate and underserved-student performance each carry 10%.

A Closer Look: America's Top 5 Public & Magnet High Schools (2026 Overview)

The five schools below lead Niche’s 2026 list, and they share a pattern: all five are public, all five select their students, and four of the five are built around science and mathematics. Each profile gives the verified facts — founding, model, size and admissions — from the school’s own published pages.

1. North Carolina School of Science and Mathematics (NCSSM) – Durham, NC

Founded in 1980 as the nation’s first public residential STEM high school, the North Carolina School of Science and Mathematics is a constituent institution of the University of North Carolina system. It is a two-year, tuition-free residential school for juniors and seniors, with 680 students on its Durham campus and 300 on its Morganton campus, which opened for the 2022–23 school year. Classes are taught by teachers with advanced degrees. Niche has named it the best public high school in America three times in four years, and the top public high school in North Carolina for six years running.

2. The Davidson Academy – Reno, NV

The Davidson Academy, established in 2006, is a free public school for profoundly gifted students on the campus of the University of Nevada, Reno. Admission requires a score in the 99.9th percentile on accepted intelligence or achievement tests. There are no grade levels: students are grouped by ability, each follows a Prospective Learning Plan, and older students take university courses through dual enrolment at UNR, including graduate-level classes. An online campus, launched in the 2017–18 school year, extends the same model beyond Reno.

3. Massachusetts Academy of Math & Science (Mass Academy) – Worcester, MA

The Massachusetts Academy of Math and Science was established in 1992 by the Massachusetts Legislature as a public school of excellence for 100 academically accelerated juniors and seniors — 50 in each grade. About 50 students join the junior class each year from a pool of more than 150 applicants, and the junior year carries more than 1,100 hours of instruction. The entire senior year is spent full-time at Worcester Polytechnic Institute, taking twelve one-term college courses plus an independent study project, with no tuition charged. It is the only public school in Massachusetts where seniors attend a private university full-time.

4. South Carolina Governor's School for Science & Mathematics (GSSM) – Hartsville, SC

The South Carolina Governor’s School for Science and Mathematics was founded in 1988 as a public, tuition-free residential school for grades 11 and 12 in Hartsville. Its first classes met on the grounds of Coker College; the school moved to its own purpose-built campus in 2003 and now houses up to 288 residential students. Every student completes the Summer Program for Research Interns — six weeks of mentored research at a university or corporate laboratory after the junior year.

5. Stuyvesant High School – New York, NY

Founded in 1904 in Manhattan, Stuyvesant High School is the flagship of New York City’s specialized high schools. Admission is decided solely by the Specialized High Schools Admissions Test (SHSAT): about 30,000 students apply and roughly 800 to 850 with the highest scores are offered places — an acceptance rate under 3%. The school enrols about 3,300 students, and four of its alumni have won Nobel Prizes.

New York, Illinois, Florida and California together contribute 45 of the 100 schools. The pattern is not a quality verdict on the other states — it reflects where selective public schools are concentrated and where the source ranking has the most complete data.

The gap is a reminder that a national ranking is a discovery tool, not a census of good schools.

Key Findings From the Top 100 high schools in the USA

  1. The list is dominated by selective STEM, exam, magnet and early-college models. That pattern is meaningful: high average outcomes partly reflect who can enter, not only what happens after enrolment.
  2. State tests are designed within different accountability systems. A national ordering can help discovery, but the margin between two schools should never be read as a precise difference in teaching quality.
  3. The 2026–27 U.S. News top ten changed materially, with Central Magnet School leading its release. Families should therefore record the publisher and edition whenever they quote a rank.
  4. The list is also geographically narrow: 45 of the 100 schools sit in four states, and 26 states have no school on it at all. A family in one of those states is not looking at a worse school system — it is looking at a list that was never designed to cover every state equally.

Five Admissions Routes: How Students Actually Get In

The schools in this table do not share one admissions route, and the route matters as much as the rank: a school you cannot attend is not on your list, however high it sits. The five routes below cover every school in the top 100.

1. Statewide residential schools

Schools such as the North Carolina School of Science and Mathematics and the South Carolina Governor’s School recruit across their whole state and select by application and merit review. Students live on campus for their junior and senior years, and tuition is free. Students apply before their junior year; the school, not the home address, decides.

2. Exam-based specialized schools

New York City’s specialized high schools admit on a single test, the Specialized High Schools Admissions Test (SHSAT). About 30,000 students sit the test and roughly 800 to 850 with the highest scores are offered a place at Stuyvesant — under 3%. The score and the student’s ranking of the schools decide the offer; grades, essays and interviews do not.

3. Magnet schools

Magnet schools such as Walter Payton College Prep in Chicago, the School for the Talented and Gifted in Dallas and Bergen County Academies in New Jersey admit by application: grades, test scores, auditions or interviews, depending on the school’s focus. Some serve a city, others a county or region — check the eligibility area before applying.

4. Charter schools

Charter schools such as the BASIS campuses and the Signature School are public and free, and they admit by open enrolment. When more students apply than there are places, a lottery decides. There is no entrance exam, but there may be a waitlist.

5. Attendance-zone comprehensive schools

Schools such as Scarsdale Senior High School, New Trier Township High School and Jericho Senior High School admit the students who live inside their district boundary. Admission is a matter of address, not application — verify the boundary with the district’s own tool, not a real-estate listing.

Admissions to Top High Schools in the USA

The schools in this table do not share one admissions route. State residential schools may recruit statewide and require an application; exam or magnet schools may use tests, grades, auditions or lotteries; charter schools can use lotteries; and comprehensive schools commonly use an attendance boundary. Before a family compares course catalogues, it should answer a simpler question: is the student legally and practically eligible to attend?

For each shortlist school, record the operator, eligible grades, residency rule, application window, selection method and transport responsibility. If an address determines eligibility, verify it with the district's current boundary tool. Do not assume that a real-estate listing, school-review platform or old boundary map is authoritative.

Timing matters as much as eligibility. The SHSAT is taken in the autumn of eighth grade and offers arrive the following March. The residential governor’s schools take students for their junior and senior years, so applications go in during the sophomore year. Magnet and charter deadlines are set by each district or school — put every deadline for a shortlist school on one calendar before comparing anything else.

Public-school rank versus student fit

A highly selective STEM school can be exceptional for a student who wants accelerated mathematics and a peer group built around research. It may be a poor fit for a student who needs a broad arts timetable, a neighbourhood sports culture or a less compressed workload. Course access, counselling, belonging and sustainable travel deserve at least as much attention as a national badge.

How to Choose From the Top 100 high schools in the USA

  • Confirm whether the school is residential, statewide, magnet, charter or attendance-zone based.
  • Compare course access—not just course count—including AP, IB, dual enrolment, arts and career pathways.
  • Ask for subgroup outcomes, counselling load, student support, commute and four-year retention.
  • Read the latest school profile and district accountability report before treating a ranking badge as current.

A five-step family decision process

  1. Set non-negotiables: eligible grade, location, curriculum, language, day or boarding, support and maximum all-in cost.
  2. Check access: catchment, passport, test, interview, prerequisite, visa and deadline rules.
  3. Verify evidence: use official or accreditor sources and record the date, cohort and definition behind every statistic.
  4. Test daily fit: model travel, timetable, workload, residence life, extracurricular access and student support.
  5. Compare offers: evaluate the written place, conditions, total cost, aid, refund terms and transition plan—not the ranking number alone.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the number one high school in the USA?

It depends on the publisher and edition. Niche’s 2026 public-school list places North Carolina School of Science and Mathematics first; U.S. News places Central Magnet School first in its separate 2026–27 methodology.

Does this top 100 include private high schools?

No. The comparison is intentionally public-school focused, including magnets, charters, residential public schools and traditional public schools.

How do students get into the top public high schools?

There is no single route. Statewide residential schools select by application and merit review; New York City’s specialized schools admit solely on the SHSAT; magnet schools use grades, tests, auditions or interviews; charter schools use open enrolment and lotteries; and attendance-zone schools admit by home address. Before comparing course catalogues, check whether the student is eligible to attend at all.

Why are so many magnet and STEM schools near the top?

Selective admissions, concentrated advanced coursework and specialized missions can produce strong measured outcomes. Those advantages also make direct comparison with open-enrolment schools imperfect.

Can an international student attend a U.S. public high school?

Sometimes, but visa, district, exchange-program and tuition rules apply. Families should confirm eligibility with the district and an authorized immigration source.
