McGill vs University of Toronto: Canada Comparison (2026)

McGill vs University of Toronto: 2026-27 international tuition ($54,000 vs $66,110), January 2027 deadlines, enrolment and grade requirements.

Tables, images and URL checked 13 November 2026 · Verify time-sensitive facts

McGill and the University of Toronto are Canada's two heavyweight research universities, and for international applicants they are usually the first two names on the list. Unlike Oxford and Cambridge, there is no rule stopping you from applying to both — but the choice between them is still a genuine one, because almost everything about the two institutions runs in opposite directions. Toronto is enormous: 103,140 students across three campuses in Fall 2025. McGill, at 40,531 students, is less than half that size and concentrated in one downtown. Their 2026-27 international tuition differs by more than $12,000 a year for an arts degree, their fee models work differently (one locks your rate, the other steps it up), and they sit in two very different cities — one of them French-speaking, though McGill itself teaches in English. This page compares them on the figures the two universities publish themselves: enrolment, tuition, deadlines, grade expectations and living costs.

Everything below is drawn from mcgill.ca and utoronto.ca — quick-facts pages, official fee schedules and admissions pages — checked on 13 November 2026 and listed in full at the end. One honesty note before we start: neither university publishes an official acceptance rate, so you will not find one here; what you will find is the grade data both universities do publish. Start with the two profiles for the full picture: McGill University and University of Toronto.

A closer look at both universities

McGill University — Montreal, Quebec

McGill packs 40,531 students (28,167 of them undergraduates) into a downtown Montreal campus at the foot of Mount Royal, plus the Macdonald campus in Sainte-Anne-de-Bellevue. It describes itself in its own materials as Canada's top-ranked medical-doctoral university, and the research intensity is measurable in its student mix: doctoral students make up 12.1 per cent of its degree-seeking population, against a 6.7 per cent average across Canadian research universities. International students are 27 per cent of the student body by origin — 29.5 per cent of degree-seeking students. The city's language is French, but McGill's main language of instruction is English, and students may write exams and assignments in English or French. The full admissions picture is in our McGill admissions guide.

University of Toronto — Toronto, Ontario

U of T is a different scale of institution: 103,140 students in total (81,412 undergraduates) spread over three campuses — St. George in downtown Toronto (69,921 students), Mississauga (17,361) and Scarborough (15,858). Arts & Science students at St. George also belong to one of seven colleges (Innis, New, St. Michael's, Trinity, University, Victoria and Woodsworth), a small-community layer inside the big university. U of T points to $1.73 billion in sponsored research funding and calls itself Canada's top university; 27,897 of its students are international, from 176 countries. Which campus and faculty you enter matters enormously — the three campuses are effectively three mid-sized universities sharing one name. Our Toronto admissions guide walks through them.

Applying: two systems, the same January crunch

For fall 2027 entry, both universities land their main deadline in the same week — but through different doors. Toronto applications run through Ontario's OUAC system: the application deadline is 15 January 2027 (the university recommends applying by 7 November 2026), with supporting documents due 15 January for Engineering and Music and 1 February for everything else. McGill runs its own application: international applicants apply by 15 January, with supporting documents due 1 March and the Major Entrance Scholarship deadline on 21 January. Neither university interviews for general undergraduate admission; the file — grades above all — does the work.

What grades actually get in. Both universities publish expectations rather than rates, and both sets are demanding. U of T publishes "approximate grade requirements" by programme: Computer Science at St. George sits in the low 90s, Computer Science at Mississauga in the high 80s, Management at Scarborough in the mid 80s — and the university notes that roughly two-thirds of its new students arrive with averages of 90 per cent or above. McGill publishes minimum Top-6 grade ranges (shown here for Ontario applicants): Arts in the high 80s to low 90s, Science's Biological and Biomedical stream in the low to mid 90s, and Bioengineering in the mid to high 90s. McGill also publishes its raw Fall 2026 counts — 41,836 applicants, 18,782 offers, an entering class of 7,343 — which tells you the shape of the funnel without pretending to be a single acceptance rate.

Applying to both in one cycle, step by step

  1. Shortlist by programme and campus first: at Toronto, the campus and faculty are separate choices (St. George is not Mississauga); at McGill, you apply to a specific faculty or program.
  2. Open both applications in the autumn: OUAC for Toronto, McGill's own portal for McGill. Toronto recommends submitting by 7 November 2026 even though the deadline is later.
  3. File both by 15 January 2027 (McGill's international application deadline is 15 January), and note McGill's entrance-scholarship deadline of 21 January.
  4. Send supporting documents on each university's own clock: Toronto by 15 January (Engineering, Music) or 1 February (others); McGill by 1 March.
  5. Prepare your English evidence if you need it — Toronto's published IELTS floor is 6.5 overall with no band below 6.0 — and compare any offers on total cost, using the fee models below, not the first-year sticker price.

Costs: a $12,000 gap, and two different fee models

For 2026-27 international tuition, McGill is the cheaper of the two at every comparable point: BA $54,000; BSc $64,785.90; BEng $65,167.50; BCom (Desautels) $71,687.40. Toronto charges $66,110 for Arts & Science and $72,860 for Applied Science & Engineering, with Rotman Commerce starting at $66,110 in Year 1 and stepping up to $75,150 for the 2025 cohort in 2026-27. The structural difference matters as much as the sticker: McGill runs a Guaranteed Tuition Model — the rate you start on is locked for the duration of your programme, provided you stay enrolled and do not transfer programmes. Toronto's fees step up as you progress (continuing-student increases are capped at 5 per cent a year), so a Toronto degree's later years cost more than its first. Housing narrows the gap a little: McGill's residences run about $20,000 a year including a meal plan (off-campus apartments $9,000–$12,000), while Toronto residence runs $10,000–$16,000 plus a meal plan of $5,100–$5,825. Add books at roughly $1,000–$2,000 a year at either. Budget with the universities' own components — our Top 25 Universities in Canada guide puts both in the national cost picture.

Montreal vs Toronto: the student-life difference

Toronto is Canada's largest city and its business capital: internships, part-time work and post-graduation employers are on U of T's doorstep, and the St. George campus is woven through downtown rather than fenced off from it. The price is big-city cost and big-university anonymity — with 17,668 new first-entry undergraduates arriving in a single fall, you have to build your own small world (the college system at St. George, and the smaller Mississauga and Scarborough campuses, exist partly for that). Montreal is the smaller, cheaper, more student-saturated city, and McGill's downtown campus gives it a more contained, campus-centred undergraduate life. The French question is simpler than most families expect: McGill teaches in English and your work can be written in English or French — the famous exception is Law, whose BCL/JD is a bilingual programme. Day-to-day Montreal life rewards some French and most students pick it up; admission to McGill (Law aside) does not require it.

Use a decision framework, not a winner label

Work through four questions. First, scale: do you want a 40,000-student university where the undergraduate core shares one downtown campus, or a 103,000-student system where your real home is one campus and one faculty among three cities' worth of choice? Second, money over four years, not one: McGill starts cheaper and locks the rate; Toronto starts higher and steps up — model the full degree at both before the gap surprises you. Third, city as curriculum: Toronto's employer density against Montreal's cheaper, more student-centred life — and, for some students, the bonus of graduating with working French from daily life rather than a classroom. Fourth, programme specifics: the published grade ranges and the exact faculty — a Scarborough Management offer and a St. George Computer Science offer are both "Toronto" but they are different universities in practice, just as McGill's faculties admit on very different grade lines. The brand names are peers; the four answers rarely are.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I apply to both McGill and the University of Toronto?

Yes. There is no either-or rule like Oxbridge's. Toronto applications go through Ontario's OUAC system (deadline 15 January 2027 for fall 2027, with 7 November 2026 recommended), while McGill runs its own application (international deadline 15 January, documents by 1 March). Many applicants file both in the same cycle and decide when offers arrive.

Which is cheaper for an international student?

McGill, on tuition at every comparable point in 2026-27: BA $54,000 against Toronto Arts & Science at $66,110, and BEng $65,167.50 against Toronto Engineering at $72,860. The gap widens over a degree because McGill's Guaranteed Tuition Model locks your starting rate for the programme's duration, while Toronto's fees step up year to year (continuing increases capped at 5 per cent annually). Housing partly offsets it: McGill residences cost about $20,000 including a meal plan, while Toronto residence plus meal plan runs roughly $15,100–$21,825.

Which is harder to get into?

Neither university publishes an official acceptance rate, so be wary of any site that quotes one. On published grade expectations they are peers at the top end: Toronto's Computer Science at St. George asks for low 90s and about two-thirds of Toronto entrants hold averages of 90 per cent or more; McGill's minimum Top-6 ranges run from high 80s–low 90s for Arts to mid–high 90s for Bioengineering. McGill's Fall 2026 funnel — 41,836 applicants, 18,782 offers, 7,343 entering — shows the volume both operate at. Your programme, not the university average, decides your odds.

Do I need French to study at McGill?

No. McGill's main language of instruction is English, and students may write exams and assignments in English or French. Montreal is a French-speaking city, so daily life is easier with some French and most students absorb it informally, but admission does not require it. The exception is Law: the BCL/JD programme is bilingual, requiring strong French comprehension.

How different are the two universities in size?

Very. In Fall 2025 Toronto enrolled 103,140 students (81,412 undergraduates) across three campuses — St. George alone (69,921) is bigger than all of McGill, which enrolled 40,531 (28,167 undergraduates) mainly on one downtown campus. Toronto counters its scale with structure: three distinct campuses and, for Arts & Science at St. George, membership of one of seven colleges. McGill's undergraduate life is correspondingly more concentrated in one place.
