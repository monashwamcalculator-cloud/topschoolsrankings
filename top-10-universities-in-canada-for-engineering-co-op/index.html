Engineering universities in Canada are globally renowned for combining rigorous academic accredited degrees (CEAB) with structured paid Co-operative Education (Co-op) work terms. For international students choosing between global study destinations, Canada offers a unique blend of world-ranked engineering faculties, industry-integrated paid internships, and eligible Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) pathways, as outlined in our comprehensive Top 25 Universities in Canada for International Students guide.

1. Why Choose Co-op Engineering in Canada?

In Canada, engineering degrees are regulated by the Canadian Engineering Accreditation Board (CEAB). A key advantage for international students is the integration of Co-operative Education:

  • Paid Work Terms: Co-op students complete 4 to 6 work terms (4 months each) or a continuous 12-to-16-month Professional Experience Year (PEY). Engineering co-op salaries in Canada average $22 to $34 CAD per hour.
  • Work Experience towards P.Eng: Work terms completed under a licensed Professional Engineer (P.Eng) can count towards the 4 years of experience required for professional licensure in provinces like Ontario and BC.
  • PGWP Eligibility: Full-time undergraduate engineering graduates from recognized Designated Learning Institutions (DLIs) qualify for up to a 3-year Post-Graduation Work Permit.

2. Deep Dive: Top 5 Engineering Schools in Detail

1. University of Waterloo (Faculty of Engineering)

Waterloo operates the largest post-secondary co-op program in the world. Engineering at Waterloo is strictly co-op only—students alternate between 4 months of study and 4 months of paid work from their first year onward. Key specializations include Software Engineering, Mechatronics, Biomedical, and Systems Design.

2. University of Toronto (Faculty of Applied Science & Engineering)

U of T Engineering is Canada's top-ranked engineering faculty globally. Instead of short 4-month terms, U of T offers the Professional Experience Year (PEY) Co-op, allowing students to work continuously for 12 to 16 months with major employers like Apple, AMD, Tesla, and Hydro One before their final year.

3. University of British Columbia (UBC Faculty of Applied Science)

Located in Vancouver, UBC Engineering combines strong research with an extensive co-op option. UBC is particularly noted for Civil, Environmental, Chemical, and Computer Engineering, maintaining strong recruitment pipelines into Pacific Northwest tech and sustainability sectors.

4. McGill University (Faculty of Engineering)

McGill in Montreal offers prestigious engineering programs with strong industrial ties across aerospace (Bombardier, CAE) and AI research. While co-op is optional for most tracks, mining and materials engineering feature mandatory co-op streams.

5. University of Alberta (Faculty of Engineering)

U of A hosts one of Canada's premier energy and natural resources engineering departments. Its co-op engineering stream places over 1,400 students annually across Alberta's booming energy, infrastructure, and environmental engineering sectors.

3. Admission Requirements & Prerequisites

Canadian undergraduate engineering programs assess applicants primarily on Grade 12 high school academic performance in core subjects:

  • Required Subjects: Advanced Functions / Calculus, Chemistry, Physics, and Grade 12 English.
  • Grade Thresholds: Competitive admission averages range from mid-80s (UCalgary, Concordia) to mid-90s (Waterloo Software/Mechatronics, UofT Engineering Science).
  • Supplemental Applications: Top programs (Waterloo AIF, UofT Online Student Profile) require mandatory video interviews and personal statement essays.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Which Canadian university has the best co-op program for engineering?

The University of Waterloo is widely recognized as having Canada's strongest engineering co-op program, partnering with over 7,000 employers globally. University of Toronto's PEY program is also highly regarded for long-term continuous placements.

How much can students earn during an engineering co-op in Canada?

Co-op hourly wages vary by discipline and region, typically ranging between $22 CAD and $34 CAD per hour. Over 4 to 5 co-op terms, engineering students can earn between $40,000 and $70,000 CAD to offset tuition costs.

Are international engineering graduates eligible for PGWP in Canada?

Yes. Graduates of accredited full-time bachelor's degree programs in engineering from Canadian DLIs are eligible for a 3-year Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) upon graduation.
