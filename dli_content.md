Check the linked official source again before applying or paying.</div><section><h2>What DLI status tells you</h2><p>A designated learning institution is a school approved by a provincial or territorial government to host international students. EduCanada advises international applicants to confirm that a postsecondary school is on the DLI list because a letter of acceptance from a DLI is needed for a study-permit application.</p><p>DLI status alone does not guarantee admission, a study permit, work eligibility, a post-graduation work permit or a particular immigration result. Those questions have separate rules that can change.</p></section><section><h2>Match more than the school name</h2><p>Similar institution names and multi-campus providers make careful matching important. If an agent or offer letter uses a trading name, ask the school to confirm the legal name and DLI number in writing.</p><ul><li>Use the legal institution name shown in the government list.</li><li>Check the province or territory and the specific campus.</li><li>Match the DLI number with the institution’s official information.</li><li>Confirm that your exact programme is currently offered at that campus.</li><li>Check the programme start date, delivery mode and credential.</li></ul></section><section><h2>Verify programme and cost information directly</h2><p>EduCanada’s programme search can help build a shortlist, but its own disclaimer tells students to confirm programmes and tuition with the institution. Open the official school page, check the current intake and request a full list of compulsory charges and refund rules.</p><p>Save the offer, fee schedule and relevant official pages. Do not rely only on screenshots forwarded through messaging apps or an agent-created brochure.</p></section><section><h2>Keep immigration checks separate</h2><p>Use Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada for current study-permit and post-study rules. Requirements can vary by programme, credential, campus and date. Avoid any adviser who presents admission as a guaranteed route to work or permanent residence.</p></section><section><h2>Payment safety checklist</h2><ul><li>Pay only through an institution-approved channel.</li><li>Confirm the recipient name and currency before transferring funds.</li><li>Read deposit and tuition-refund conditions before payment.</li><li>Keep receipts and written communication.</li><li>Pause if you are pressured to pay before discrepancies are resolved.</li></ul></section><section><h2>Recheck before the study-permit submission</h2><p>Statuses and rules can change between research, admission and the visa application. Recheck the DLI entry, school page and current government instructions immediately before submitting, and use the date on the page rather than assuming an older saved copy is current.</p></section><section class="faq-section"><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2><details><summary>Does DLI status guarantee a Canadian study permit?</summary><p>No. It is one part of eligibility; the government assesses the full application under current rules.</p></details><details><summary>Does every programme at a DLI have the same post-study eligibility?</summary><p>No. Programme, credential, campus and other current conditions can matter, so check the official government guidance for the exact case.</p></details><details><summary>Can I rely on an education agent’s DLI screenshot?</summary><p>Use it only as a lead.
