Top 10 Hardest Boarding Schools to Get Into in the USA (2026 Guide)

The top 10 hardest boarding schools to get into in the USA for 2026 by reported selectivity: Groton, Exeter, Andover, St. Paul's, Hotchkiss, Deerfield, Lawrenceville, Choate, Milton, and Cate.

Overview: Elite boarding schools do not publish official acceptance rate reports the way colleges do through Common Data Set filings, so every “hardest boarding schools” list — including this one — relies on historical estimates from admissions consultants and school-reported figures. Based on the most consistently cited approximate rates, this guide ranks the top 10 hardest boarding schools to get into in the USA, led by Groton School and the “Sixteen Schools”-tier group including Exeter, Andover, and St. Paul’s. Treat every figure below as an approximate historical range, not a guaranteed current-year statistic.

Unlike colleges, U.S. boarding schools are not required to disclose admissions statistics publicly, and most decline to confirm exact acceptance rates. The figures circulating online — including in this article — are compiled by independent admissions consultants (such as TABS-affiliated advisors, Command Education, and Ivy Coach) from school-reported data, applicant surveys, and historical reporting. Always verify current numbers directly with each school’s admissions office before treating any rate as fact.

Why boarding school acceptance rates are hard to verify

  • No Common Data Set requirement: Unlike colleges, secondary boarding schools are not required to file standardized admissions data.
  • Schools rarely confirm exact figures: Most elite boarding schools decline to publish a precise acceptance rate each year.
  • Applicant pool overlap: Many strong applicants apply to several of the same ten-to-fifteen schools, so a school’s rate can shift with total applicant volume, not just quality.
  • Estimates vary by source: Different consultants and forums report slightly different numbers for the same school and year.

Why Groton is often the single hardest boarding school to get into

Groton School consistently appears at or near the top of selectivity lists for a structural reason: it is one of the smallest schools among the historically prominent New England boarding schools, enrolling roughly 375–400 students total across four grades. A tiny total enrollment means very few open seats each year relative to a large, national and international applicant pool — which mechanically produces a lower acceptance rate than larger peer schools, independent of any difference in application quality.

This list vs. general “best boarding school” rankings

Selectivity and overall reputation are related but not identical. A school can be extremely hard to get into (low acceptance rate) without being ranked #1 on general aggregator sites like Niche, which weight academics, athletics, food, campus, and student reviews together. This list is intentionally ordered by reported selectivity alone — cross-check it against our companion guide on STEM-focused boarding schools, which ranks a similar but not identical group of schools by a completely different criterion (science and math program depth).

Waitlists and reapplying: what families should know

  • Waitlist movement varies widely by year — some years a school pulls a meaningful number of students off the waitlist after May 1 college-style deposit deadlines shift enrollment; other years almost none move.
  • Reapplying to the same school for a later entry grade is possible at most of these schools, though admissions offices generally recommend a materially stronger application (grades, testing, or activities) the second time.
  • Financial aid availability can affect waitlist movement, since some schools manage aid budgets alongside admission offers.

How to strengthen a boarding school application

  1. Start early — most applications open in the fall of the year before enrollment, with January deadlines common via the Standard Application Online (SAO).
  2. Take the SSAT or ISEE seriously — most of these schools require one of these standardized tests.
  3. Visit or interview genuinely — admissions officers at small schools notice generic, copy-paste interest.
  4. Show depth, not breadth, in extracurriculars — one or two areas of real commitment outperform a long shallow list.
  5. Apply to a realistic range of 6–10 schools rather than only the very hardest names on this list.

For a detailed, school-specific walkthrough, see our how to get into Phillips Exeter Academy guide — much of its strategy (SSAT prep timeline, interview approach, essay focus) applies broadly to the other schools on this list as well.

Regional vs. national and international applicant pools

Selectivity at these ten schools is also shaped by how broad an applicant pool they draw from:

  • National reach: All ten schools recruit actively across the United States, not just from the Northeast, which widens the applicant pool considerably compared to a typical regional private day school.
  • International enrollment: Most of these schools enroll international boarding students from 20–40+ countries, adding further competition for a fixed number of boarding seats.
  • Geographic diversity goals: Admissions offices often aim for balanced representation across U.S. regions and countries, which can affect an individual applicant’s odds depending on where they live — a factor that is rarely quantified publicly but is commonly acknowledged by admissions consultants.
  • Feeder relationships: Some middle schools and educational consultants have long-standing relationships with specific boarding schools, which can (unofficially) influence application volume from certain regions or programs.

Frequently asked questions: hardest boarding schools to get into in USA

What is the hardest boarding school to get into in the USA?

Groton School is commonly cited as having the lowest reported acceptance rate among major New England boarding schools, largely because of its very small total enrollment (roughly 375–400 students).

Are boarding school acceptance rates officially published?

No. Unlike colleges, boarding schools are not required to publish standardized admissions data. Figures cited in articles like this one come from admissions consultants, school-reported estimates, and historical reporting — always confirm current numbers with the school directly.

Is Phillips Exeter or Phillips Academy Andover harder to get into?

Historical reporting places both schools in a similar, very competitive range (roughly 13–18%), with year-to-year fluctuation. Neither is consistently and reliably harder than the other across all reported sources.

Why isn’t this list the same order as Niche’s “best boarding schools” ranking?

Niche and similar aggregators weight academics, campus life, athletics, and student/parent reviews together. This list is ordered specifically by reported selectivity (acceptance rate), which is a narrower and different measure than overall reputation.

What test do I need to apply to these boarding schools?

Most require the SSAT or ISEE. Some schools have adjusted testing policies in recent admissions cycles, so confirm current requirements directly with each school before your application cycle.

Do these schools offer financial aid to international students?

Some do, though international aid is often more limited or need-aware compared to domestic aid policies. Groton, Exeter, and Andover are generally cited as having relatively strong need-based aid programs — verify current international aid policy directly with each school.

How many boarding schools should I apply to if I want a competitive shot?

Admissions consultants commonly recommend a balanced list of 6–10 schools spanning a range of selectivity levels, rather than applying only to the handful of hardest schools on this list.

Is a lower acceptance rate always a sign of a “better” school?

Not necessarily. A low acceptance rate often reflects small enrollment and applicant pool size (as with Groton) rather than a definitive quality gap versus a slightly less selective peer school. Fit, program strength, and financial aid matter as much as the acceptance rate itself.

Do these schools have waitlists, and do they move?

Yes, all of these schools use waitlists, and movement varies significantly by year depending on how many admitted students enroll elsewhere. Some years see meaningful waitlist movement after the enrollment deadline; other years see very little. Contact the admissions office directly for realistic expectations in a given cycle.

When do decisions come out for these boarding schools?

Most of these schools release decisions in mid-March, with an enrollment reply deadline typically in early April, though exact dates shift slightly each year and are set independently by each school’s admissions office.

Final overview

The hardest boarding schools to get into in the USA — led by Groton, Exeter, Andover, and St. Paul’s — combine small class sizes, global applicant pools, and historically reported acceptance rates in the roughly 9%–20% range. Because none of these figures are officially published annually, treat every number here as a directional estimate, build a realistic school list of 6–10 institutions, and confirm current admissions data directly with each school.
