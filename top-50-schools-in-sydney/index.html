Top 50 Schools in Sydney

Sydney’s 50 highest-performing schools ranked by 2025 HSC success rate — Band 6 results as a share of exams sat — with selective and independent schools clearly labelled, the full table, and parent FAQs.

Published November 13, 2026 · Reviewed by the TSR Editorial Desk · Official sources linked

Top 50 Schools in Sydney: The 2026 Guide

Overview / quick answer: This list ranks Sydney schools by one published measure: the 2025 HSC success rate, which is the share of a school’s exam entries that earned a Band 6 or E4 result (a mark of 90 or above). North Sydney Boys High School ranks first at 71.60%, ahead of James Ruse Agricultural High School at 70.43%. Seventeen of the top 50 are government selective schools and 33 are independent schools. The full table, the method, and the limits of this measure are set out below.

Every year, parents ask the same question in a dozen different ways: which Sydney school gets the best results? There is no single official league table for Sydney schools, and no ranking can measure everything a school does. What does exist is a consistent, published set of HSC outcome data, released each year from the results recorded by the NSW Education Standards Authority (NESA). This guide takes that data, ranks the Greater Sydney schools on it, and labels each school clearly so families can compare like with like.

The list below is a starting point for a shortlist, not a verdict on any school. Read the method section before the table, because one number — however carefully it is calculated — never tells the whole story of a school.

Top 50 Schools in Sydney: What This Ranking Means

“Top” in this guide means one specific thing: the school’s Year 12 cohort converted a high share of its HSC exam entries into top-band results in 2025. It does not mean the first school is the right school for every child, and a difference of one or two places is not educationally significant. Cohorts differ, subjects differ, and schools differ in what they offer beyond exam results.

The table is designed for clear comparison: each school’s name, sector and success rate are stated directly, along with its rank across all of New South Wales, so schools with similar names are easy to tell apart and no school hides behind vague wording.

How We Ranked the Top 50 Schools in Sydney

The ranking uses the HSC success rate published from NESA’s 2025 results data: the number of Band 6 and E4 results (marks of 90 or above, known as Distinguished Achiever results) divided by the total number of HSC exam entries at the school, expressed as a percentage. We took the published NSW table and cross-checked the figures between two independent published compilations of the same NESA data — Matrix Education’s 2025 high school rankings and New College’s Top 100 Schools HSC table — before publishing. Where the two compilations were compared, the figures agreed.

“Sydney” in this guide means Greater Sydney. Two schools that sit high in the NSW table are not in this list because they are outside Greater Sydney: Merewether High School (NSW rank 22, Newcastle) and Gosford High School (NSW rank 33, Central Coast). Every school in the NSW top 21 is a Sydney school.

Sector labels follow the published source classification. “Government selective” means a NSW public school that selects students by competitive entry. “Independent” follows the source tables’ classification and includes Catholic schools. No school paid for inclusion or position, and no position was changed for any reason other than the published figures.

A Closer Look: Sydney’s Top 5 Schools (2025 HSC)

1. North Sydney Boys High School — 71.60%

North Sydney Boys, a government selective school in Crows Nest, recorded the highest success rate in New South Wales in 2025: 663 top-band results from 926 exam entries, a rate of 71.60%. It is the third consecutive year the school has topped the NSW table. Families should note what sits behind the number — entry is by competitive selective placement, so the cohort starts from a selected intake. Our James Ruse vs Baulkham Hills comparison explains how selective entry shapes results like these.

2. James Ruse Agricultural High School — 70.43%

James Ruse, in Carlingford, finished a close second at 70.43%, with 705 top-band results — more than any other selective school in the state. The school is consistently at or near the top of the NSW table, and demand for places is intense. Our full James Ruse entry guide covers how admission works and what the results record shows.

3. Sydney Grammar School — 60.99%

Sydney Grammar is the highest-ranked independent school in Sydney and in NSW, at 60.99%. It also recorded the highest number of Band 6 results of any school in the state — 763 from 1,251 exam entries — reflecting one of the largest cohorts in the top group. See the school’s Sydney Grammar profile for its programme and admissions details.

4. North Sydney Girls High School — 60.46%

North Sydney Girls, the selective girls’ school in Crows Nest, ranked fourth at 60.46% (549 top-band results from 908 entries). Together with North Sydney Boys, it gives one suburb the two highest-ranked single-sex selective schools in the state.

5. Normanhurst Boys High School — 57.73%

Normanhurst Boys rounds out the top five at 57.73%. Its cohort of 124 HSC students was the smallest among the top five, which makes its rate the more notable — smaller cohorts usually swing more from year to year, and this school has held a top-five position on the strength of its 2025 results.

Where the Top 50 Schools in Sydney Are Located

The top of the list clusters where Sydney’s selective schools and long-established independent schools have always been: the North Shore, the Hills District, the inner city and the Eastern Suburbs. But the list is not only a North Shore story. Penrith High School (17th) represents the city’s west, Hurlstone Agricultural High School (41st) and the Parramatta schools represent the centre-west, and Caringbah High School (30th) represents the Sutherland Shire in the south.

Selective schools draw students from across the whole city, because entry is by test rather than by catchment. Independent schools mostly draw from their surrounding districts. For most families, the practical question is not which school is first on this page but which strong school their child can actually travel to each day — a point we return to in the choosing section below.

Key Findings From the Top 50 Schools in Sydney

  • Sydney holds the entire NSW top 21. The first school outside Greater Sydney appears at NSW rank 22.
  • Four schools cleared 60%. North Sydney Boys (71.60%), James Ruse (70.43%), Sydney Grammar (60.99%) and North Sydney Girls (60.46%) are the only Sydney schools above that line in 2025.
  • Selective schools lead, independent schools fill the list. Seventeen of the 50 are government selective schools — including seven of the top ten — and 33 are independent schools. No non-selective government school makes the Sydney top 50 on this measure; the highest-ranked one sits outside it at NSW rank 57.
  • The most top-band results came from an independent school. Sydney Grammar’s 763 Band 6 results were the highest count in NSW, ahead of Knox Grammar School (724, at Sydney rank 28) and James Ruse (705) — a reminder that big cohorts produce big counts even at lower rates.
  • The gap from first to fiftieth is wide. The list runs from 71.60% down to 26.86% (Newington College), so “top 50” covers very different results profiles.
  • Small cohorts need care. Conservatorium High School ranks 15th from just 40 HSC students; a handful of results either way would move a school of that size several places.

How HSC Success Rates Work — and Their Limits

In the HSC, most courses are graded in bands, and Band 6 is the highest, requiring a mark of 90 or above. In extension courses the equivalent top grade is called E4. NESA publishes lists of Distinguished Achievers — students who earn a Band 6 or E4 — and the success rate used here simply counts those results against every exam entry at the school.

Three limits matter. First, selective schools choose their students by academic test, so their rates partly reflect their intake, not only their teaching. Second, the rate says nothing about the middle of the cohort: a school can lift many students from average to strong without producing many Band 6s. Third, one year is a snapshot. Before drawing conclusions about any school, look at several years of results, the subjects your child wants to study, and the school’s own published information.

How to Choose From the Top 50 Schools in Sydney

Start with access. For a government selective school, your child must sit the selective placement process and score high enough for an offer — our NSW selective school exam guide explains the process and timeline. For an independent school, check the fees, the enrolment timeline (some schools take applications years ahead) and what the fees include, using the school’s own published fee schedule rather than any third-party figure.

Then filter by fit: the commute your child can manage twice a day, the subjects and activities on offer, and the school’s approach to students who need support or extension. Schools such as Baulkham Hills High School publish detailed entry information that is worth reading in full before applying. Finally, treat this ranking as one input. FAQs About the Top 50 Schools in Sydney

Which is the number one school in Sydney?

On 2025 HSC success rate, North Sydney Boys High School ranks first in Sydney and in NSW, at 71.60%. James Ruse Agricultural High School is second at 70.43% and recorded more Band 6 results (705) than any other selective school.

Are the top 50 schools in Sydney public or private?

Both. Seventeen are government selective schools, which are public schools with entry by competitive test. The other 33 are independent schools, a category that in the source data includes Catholic schools.

What is a good HSC success rate?

It depends on the school’s intake. In this list, the top 50 runs from 71.60% down to 26.86%, and four schools exceeded 60%. A selective school and a comprehensive school should not be judged against the same expectation, because their intakes are selected differently.

Why are Merewether High School and Gosford High School not in the list?

Both ranked highly in NSW — Merewether at 22 and Gosford at 33 — but they are in Newcastle and on the Central Coast, outside Greater Sydney, so they are not part of a Sydney list.

How do students get into a selective school in Sydney?

By sitting the NSW selective high schools placement process while in primary school and receiving an offer based on the outcome. Our selective school exam guide walks through eligibility, test format and key dates.

When will this Sydney ranking be updated?

After NESA publishes the 2026 HSC results in December 2026, we will re-check every figure against the new published tables and update this page, recording the new fact-check date below.
