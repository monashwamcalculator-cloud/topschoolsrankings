The Brearley School: Acceptance Rate, Tuition & Admissions (2026–27)

Brearley does not publish an acceptance rate. Tuition 2026–27 is $69,600, free under $100,000. Kindergarten entry is closed; Classes V–IX apply by 1 Dec 2026.

Tables, images and URL checked 17 November 2026 · Verify time-sensitive facts

Quick summary: The Brearley School does not publish an acceptance rate, so this guide does not print one. What the school does publish is clear: tuition for 2026–27 is $69,600 a year, comprehensive; families earning less than $100,000 a year pay no tuition at all; and for 2027–28 entry, Kindergarten applications are already closed, while Classes V–IX close on 1 December 2026. Brearley is an independent all-girls day school in Manhattan, founded in 1884, teaching about 760 girls from Kindergarten to Class XII on East 83rd Street.

The Brearley School is one of New York's oldest and most sought-after girls' schools — and one of the hardest to get into, precisely because it takes so few girls at each entry point. It admits at Kindergarten and at Classes V–IX only, it takes no new girls in Classes I–IV or Class X for 2027–28, and its Kindergarten list for 2027–28 filled so fast that the school closed applications early when its interview spaces ran out. This guide leads with the school's own published dates and figures rather than with the acceptance-rate estimates that circulate on agent and aggregator sites.

Everything below comes from the school itself — brearley.org — checked on 17 November 2026, with two dated secondary figures (enrolment and faculty degrees) from Private School Review's verified school update, labelled as such. Where Brearley publishes nothing, this guide says so: there is no published acceptance rate, no published average SAT or ACT score, and no published college matriculation list. The guide covers the 2027–28 admissions timeline, the 2026–27 tuition and aid figures, how the school teaches, its campus and traditions, and what is actually known about where its graduates go.

Brearley sits in Yorkville, on Manhattan's Upper East Side, and its whole campus is three buildings within a few blocks: the historic schoolhouse at 610 East 83rd Street, which holds the Middle and Upper Schools; the second schoolhouse at 590 East 83rd Street, which holds the Lower School and opened for the 2019–20 school year; and the Field House on East 87th Street. In 2024–25 the school reported that 56% of its 772 students identified as students of color. Its mission, in the school's own words, is to challenge "girls of adventurous intellect and diverse backgrounds to think critically and creatively" and prepare them "for principled engagement in the world".

Brearley School Acceptance Rate — the Honest Answer

Brearley does not publish an acceptance rate. No rate appears anywhere on the school's admissions pages, and the percentages you will find on aggregator and consultant sites — figures around 13% or 20% — are estimates with no official source. This guide does not repeat them, because printing an estimate as a fact would mislead exactly the families who need the truth. What the school does publish, and what actually determines your chances, is the structure of the process: very few entry points, fixed deadlines, and a file that is judged as a whole.

For 2027–28, Brearley will consider applicants for Kindergarten and Classes V through IX only. It does not anticipate any openings in Classes I through IV or in Class X. Kindergarten is the largest entry point — and for 2027–28 it is already closed: the school says that because of a rise in Kindergarten applications, it no longer has parent/guardian interview spaces remaining, so no additional applicants can be considered. Families interested in a Class I–IV or Class X seat can only leave their details on an inquiry form.

Admission is not rolling. Every entry route runs on fixed ISAAGNY deadlines (see the timeline table below), and the school is explicit that decisions are made on the required pieces of a file — application, interview, school reports and, for Classes V–IX, an ISEE or SSAT score — and nothing else. It does not encourage first-choice letters (they are not shared with the admission committee), it does not consider extra materials beyond what is required, and it does not accept gifts to the Admission Office. Financial aid is decided after admission, so applying for aid does not affect whether a girl is admitted; the school keeps a wait pool of qualified candidates through the end of June. Legacy means a child whose mother attended Brearley, and a sibling candidate is one whose sister is currently enrolled. A family applying to Class V or Class IX faces a small number of places, an interview, a standardised test and teacher references, with a single deadline — 1 December 2026 — that is now two weeks away. There is no published number that turns that into odds, and any site that gives you one is guessing.

Brearley School Tuition & Financial Aid (2026–27)

Tuition for students in Kindergarten through Class XII in the 2026–27 school year is $69,600, and it is comprehensive: the school says it covers school-sponsored overnight trips, required course books and supplies, lunch, after-school co-curricular classes, clubs, after-school sports and arts programmes, field trips, orchestra and chorus, and devices (Chromebooks and laptops). There is no separate bill for those items — the point of the comprehensive figure is that the number you see is the number you pay for the school day itself.

On aid, the school's figures are just as plain. This year it will award $10 million to 21% of its students (on the basis of its 2025–26 awards). Families earning less than $100,000 a year, without significant assets, pay no tuition at all under the Free Tuition Program — the school's own calculator shows a family on $100,000 household income paying $0 tuition. Need-based grants fully meet demonstrated need, and aid extends past tuition to books, lunch, technology, athletics, trips, extracurricular activities, after-school and summer programmes, music lessons, and — for Upper School students — one travel-study programme. Applications go through Clarity, a third-party platform, and are reviewed annually.

Two practical points. The application fee is $85, and families applying for financial assistance can waive it with the code FA2026. And aid and admission run on the same calendar: for Classes V–IX the financial assistance application is due on the same day as the admission application, 1 December 2026 — so if you need aid, apply for it at the same time as admission.

1. Check whether your entry point is open

Before anything else, confirm the grade you are applying to is actually taking girls. For 2027–28 that means Kindergarten or Classes V–IX. Classes I–IV and Class X have no anticipated openings; the school offers only an inquiry form for those grades.

2. Submit the application in Ravenna and pay $85

Apply through the Ravenna portal and pay the $85 application fee. Families applying for financial assistance should use the code FA2026 when prompted, which waives the fee. Do not submit extra materials beyond what is listed — the school says additional materials will not be considered.

3. Take the ISEE or SSAT (Classes V–IX only)

Every applicant for Classes V–IX must take the ISEE or the SSAT. The school suggests registering for a test date no later than December 2026 so the file is complete by 8 January 2027. Brearley's ISEE code is 333475 and its SSAT code is 1799 — list Brearley as a school you are applying to, so results are sent directly. Kindergarten applicants do not sit a standardised test; they have a 45-minute school visit in a small group, scheduled by the Admission Office on a rolling basis with about two weeks' notice.

4. Visit, tour and interview in person

Interviews are a required part of the process for every entry route, and the school says there are no virtual alternatives for the in-person steps. For Kindergarten, the parent tour and interview run together — allow 90 minutes, with the interview immediately after the tour. For Middle and Upper School, the interview must include the student and at least one parent or guardian; a 45-minute campus tour is optional and can be taken before the interview. With advance notice, the admission team will work with families travelling to New York to coordinate a visit.

5. Send transcripts and teacher references

Transcripts are submitted through Ravenna, and from 2 November 2026 the required English and mathematics teacher reference forms are available there too. For Kindergarten, the Permission to Release Confidential School Report form is available from 15 October 2026, and the school accepts either its own form or the ISAAGNY form.

6. Wait for the decision — and know the wait-pool rule

Decisions arrive 4 February 2027 for Kindergarten (general), 9 December 2026 for early notification, 8 February 2027 for Middle School and 12 February 2027 for Class IX. If your daughter is not offered a place, she may still be held in the school's wait pool of qualified candidates, which runs through the end of June.

Academics at Brearley

Brearley's curriculum is built on the liberal arts, sequenced across thirteen years from Kindergarten to Class XII, and the school describes its own teaching as varied and flexible, "taking into account individual patterns of development and ways of learning". The Lower School (K–IV) gives a structured foundation in literacy, mathematics, science inquiry, social studies, Spanish or French, art, music and physical education, with daily writing from the start. The Middle School (V–VIII) expands into laboratory sciences, ancient and modern languages and advanced history, with an advisory programme alongside the academic load. The Upper School (IX–XII) is where the workload becomes famously heavy: analytical essays, debates, ethical case studies, seminar-style humanities and capstone independent study.

One point matters for families comparing Brearley with other schools. Brearley does not label courses AP. The school's own statement is that "there are no classes specifically at 'honors' or 'AP' level, as the regular classes prepare students for a broad range of Advanced Placement examinations" — and its published course descriptions use "Advanced" naming (Advanced Biology, Advanced Chemistry, Advanced Physics) rather than AP. So the figure of more than twenty AP courses that appears on some school-directory sites is wrong, and this guide does not repeat it. What the school does offer is depth: mathematics through calculus and statistics, triple science, and languages in Latin, French, Spanish and Mandarin Chinese. Ethics discussions, arts and service-learning run through the curriculum rather than sitting beside it, and 85% of the faculty hold advanced degrees.

The school is a member of the Interschool, a consortium of eight Manhattan independent schools — Brearley, Browning, Chapin, Collegiate, Dalton, Nightingale Bamford, Spence and Trinity — which runs joint programming through the year, including the Brearley-Collegiate Service Day from ninth grade, the Frost Valley trip in tenth grade, and shared classes such as game theory and driver's education. That is how a small school gives its girls a wider peer group.

Campus, Athletics & Arts

Brearley's campus is compact and entirely urban. The historic schoolhouse at 610 East 83rd Street holds the Middle and Upper Schools; the 85,000-square-foot second schoolhouse at 590 East 83rd Street, which opened for the 2019–20 school year, holds the Lower School and was built with sustainable initiatives; and the Field House on East 87th Street, built in the 1990s, holds regulation basketball and volleyball courts. The school also uses fields at Randall's Island and Asphalt Green for outdoor sports, and holds its annual Field Day at Randall's Island.

Athletics start in Class V, with a no-cut policy in Middle School — anyone who wants to join a team can — and in Upper School one team per season also follows a no-cut policy, while the rest are competitive and cuts take place. Brearley is a founding member of the AAIS, a league of 11 private schools across New York City. Its Upper School varsity teams, by season, are:

The arts are built into the school day rather than charged on top of it: orchestra and chorus are covered by the comprehensive tuition, and the 590 building has a multi-use, acoustically sound performance hall used by all three divisions, while the 610 building has a dance studio that the varsity dance team uses. The school's colours are red and white, its mascot is the beaver — a life-sized beaver called Bev cheers on teams at varsity games and Homecoming — and its teams compete as Red versus White, a split that dates to 1923.

History & Traditions

Samuel A. Brearley Jr. graduated from Harvard in 1871, worked as a private tutor, then studied at Balliol College, Oxford, and came to New York in 1884 — at a time, as the school's own history puts it, when it was commonly thought that intellectual activity "took the bloom from ladies" — to found a school that would give young women an education comparable to their brothers'. The school opened on 8 October 1884 at 6 East 45th Street with 41 students, aged 12 to 18, in a six-year programme. Brearley served as Head until his sudden death in 1886. The school moved first to West 44th Street, then in 1912 to Park Avenue and 61st Street, where it added a primary programme, and in 1929 it commissioned the building on 83rd Street it still occupies. It has been led by 16 Heads of School across more than 140 years.

The traditions are the part of Brearley that families remember longest. The seal, designed in 1890 and slightly redesigned in 1946, carries the lamp of truth, the book of knowledge and three beavers, and the current version has been used since the 1955–56 school year. The beaver is the mascot because it is on the seal — a life-sized beaver called Bev cheers at games — and the school motto, "By Truth and Toil", is the title of the school song, written by teacher Annie Winsor Allen in 1901. Each class in the Middle and Upper School has its own mascot — Theodore the Bear, Balthazaar the Camel, Fridgie the Penguin, Tigger the Tiger, Amelia the Duck, Socrates the Owl, Olaffub the Buffalo and Jimbo the Elephant, the first of them, bought in 1915 — and at the annual Mascot Assembly in May, a tradition dating back to at least 1923, the senior class passes its mascot to Class IV. Red versus White, the two teams every girl from Class V up is assigned to, was organised in 1923 by Edna Carling, the head of physical education; the teams compete all year, most enthusiastically at Field Day in the spring. Mountain Day, an annual autumn trip to Bear Mountain, began in 1983 under Head of School Evelyn Halpert, modelled on Smith College's tradition. The Donut Assembly brings Lower and Upper School girls together in a circle from youngest to oldest, and Last Day — the school's commencement ceremony — has been held since 1899.

College Outcomes & Alumnae

Families searching for Brearley's college matriculation usually want a list of where graduates go. The school does not publish a public matriculation list, and it publishes no average SAT or ACT score, so this guide does not print third-party estimates of either. What can be said honestly is that Brearley is known for sending its graduates — about 60 to 65 girls a year — to highly selective colleges. If a matriculation list matters to your decision, ask the Admission Office directly for the most recent graduating class's destinations; that is the only source that can give you the school's own figures.

The alumnae record is easier to verify, because the women are public figures. Caroline Kennedy attended Brearley before graduating from Concord Academy in 1975. Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney attended Brearley and went on to found the Whitney Museum of American Art in 1931. Virginia Gildersleeve graduated from Brearley in 1895, went to Barnard College, and became Barnard's long-serving dean — and the only woman the United States sent as a delegate to the 1945 San Francisco conference that wrote the United Nations Charter. Two names that appear on some school-directory sites do not belong here: Ruth Bader Ginsburg attended James Madison High School in Brooklyn, not Brearley, and Jill Abramson attended the Ethical Culture Fieldston School, not Brearley. Both were wrongly listed on the previous version of this guide, and both have been removed.

Is Brearley Right for Your Daughter?

Brearley suits a particular family, and the honest answer starts with the practical facts. It is a day school in Manhattan: every girl commutes, and the school's own arrangements show it — every student from Kindergarten to Class XII who lives at least half a mile away in New York City receives an OMNY card from the city's Department of Education, and families in Brooklyn can use a private bus run with Chapin, for an extra fee not covered by tuition. It is single-sex, K–12, with small grades of about 55–65 girls, so a girl who thrives in a close, intense peer group will fit it better than one who wants a large, anonymous school. It is academically demanding by design — heavy reading and writing, seminar discussion, and a workload the school itself does not soften. For families weighing a day school against boarding, our USA vs UK boarding schools comparison sets out what boarding adds and what it costs.

On cost, the decision is arithmetic, not guesswork. At $69,600 a year the full price is among the highest in the country, but the Free Tuition Program removes it entirely for families earning under $100,000 without significant assets, and $10 million in aid reaches 21% of students — so the right question is not the headline figure but what Clarity says your family would pay. On timing, the decision is already partly made for you: Kindergarten for 2027–28 is closed, and Classes V–IX close on 1 December 2026. If your daughter is in the right grade and you can meet that date, apply; if the grade is wrong or the date has passed, the next cycle is the honest answer, not a workaround. What are the Brearley admissions deadlines for 2027–28?

For Classes V–IX, applications and financial-aid applications are due 1 December 2026, interview registration by 7 December 2026, the completed file by 8 January 2027, decisions on 8 February 2027 (Middle School) and 12 February 2027 (Class IX), and enrolment by noon Eastern on 26 February 2027 (Middle) and 11 March 2027 (Upper). Kindergarten applications for 2027–28 are already closed. The application fee is $85, waived with the code FA2026 for families applying for aid.
