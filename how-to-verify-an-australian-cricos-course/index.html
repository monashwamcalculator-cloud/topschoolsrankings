Check an Australian provider, course code, location and duration in the official CRICOS register before applying as an international student.

How to Verify an Australian Course on CRICOS: The Complete International Student Guide

Studying in Australia offers world-class qualifications, vibrant student cities, and post-study work pathways. However, under Australian migration law, an international student holding a Subclass 500 visa can only legally enroll in education programs registered on the Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students (CRICOS).

Enrolling in an unregistered program or misinterpreting CRICOS course codes will result in an immediate student visa rejection or cancellation by the Department of Home Affairs. Furthermore, navigating recent reforms—including the Genuine Student (GS) assessment framework and tighter post-study work permit criteria—demands thorough verification prior to accepting an institutional Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE).

This official due diligence guide details how to query the CRICOS database, verify mandatory course duration for Subclass 485 visa eligibility, identify delivery campus discrepancies, and avoid unregistered private vocational traps.

What is CRICOS and Why is it Legally Mandatory?

The Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students (CRICOS) is the statutory Australian government register managed under the Education Services for Overseas Students (ESOS) Act.

  • Statutory Requirement: Every education provider recruiting international students in Australia must obtain a registered CRICOS Provider Code.
  • Individual Course Registration: Crucially, CRICOS does not merely register the university; every individual course, degree, and study location must have its own unique CRICOS course code.
  • Visa Condition Linkage: The Department of Home Affairs cross-references the Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) code directly against CRICOS before granting an Australian Student Visa (Subclass 500).

The Critical Trap: Institution Registered vs. Course Registered

A common pitfall encountered by international applicants is assuming that because a university is a renowned public institution, every single degree it offers is automatically open to international students on a student visa. This is incorrect.

Scenario CRICOS Registered? Student Visa (Subclass 500) Eligible? Regulatory Context & Warning
Standard On-Campus Degree Yes Yes Delivered face-to-face meeting full-time study requirements.
100% Online / Distance Learning No No Online courses do not qualify foreign nationals for an Australian student visa.
Part-Time Domestic Programs No No International visa holders are mandated to maintain a 100% full-time academic study load.
Offshore / Unapproved Satellite Campus Location-Dependent Conditional The specific campus location must be explicitly approved on the institution’s CRICOS schedule.

The 92-Week Rule: Protecting Your Subclass 485 Post-Study Work Visa

One of the primary benefits of completing Australian higher education is accessing the Temporary Graduate Visa (Subclass 485), which allows graduates to work unrestricted in Australia for multiple years.

To qualify for the Australian Study Requirement (ASR), candidates must fulfill the statutory 92-week rule:

  • Registered CRICOS Duration: The course (or packaged courses) must be officially registered on CRICOS for a minimum of 92 weeks of study.
  • Physical Study Period: The student must complete the qualification over a minimum of 16 calendar months in Australia.
  • The Fast-Track Trap: If an applicant completes a 2-year master’s degree registered for only 78 or 84 CRICOS weeks due to credits or accelerated trimester pacing, they may fall short of the 92-week threshold and forfeit their post-study work visa rights entirely.

Step-by-Step CRICOS Verification Protocol

Verify your program independently before paying non-refundable application or initial tuition deposit fees:

Step Verification Task Where to Check & Expected Data
Step 1 Access the Register Navigate to the official portal: cricos.education.gov.au.
Step 2 Institution Search Select "Institution Search" and enter the official legal or trading name of the university or TAFE college.
Step 3 Verify Provider Code Locate the official 5-digit alphanumeric provider code (e.g., University of Sydney is 00026A).
Step 4 Review Course Details Click "List of Courses", find your exact degree name, and inspect the specific CRICOS Course Code.
Step 5 Confirm Campus & Duration Check that your target campus location is authorized and verify the registered duration in weeks.

Recent Reforms: Genuine Student (GS) & Concurrent Enrolment

Prospective international applicants must be aware of major Australian policy updates:

1. The Genuine Student (GS) Requirement

The Australian Government has replaced the legacy Genuine Temporary Entrant (GTE) statement with the Genuine Student (GS) assessment. Rather than proving an intention to leave Australia after study, the GS framework evaluates:

  • Circumstances in the applicant's home country (economic ties, family assets, employment opportunities).
  • Value of the course to the student’s realistic future career earnings.
  • Realistic academic progression (how this course fits with previous academic history).

2. Ban on Concurrent Study / Course Swapping

In prior years, unscrupulous agents encouraged international students arriving at universities to secretly swap into low-cost vocational colleges via concurrent enrolment loopholes. The Department of Home Affairs has completely prohibited concurrent study during the first 6 months of a student’s principal course of study. Students cannot switch to a cheaper private provider prior to completing six calendar months at their primary institution without an official release letter.

Red Flags: Avoiding Fraudulent Providers & Agent Exploitation

Exercise extreme caution if you encounter any of the following operational practices:

  • No Listed CRICOS Code on Offer: An official Letter of Offer or electronic Confirmation of Enrolment (eCoE) that fails to display both a Provider CRICOS Code and a Course CRICOS Code.
  • "No Class Attendance" Guarantees: Any college promising that an international student visa holder does not need to maintain active on-campus attendance. Under the ESOS National Code, providers must report students failing minimum attendance (typically 80%) or satisfactory course progress directly to immigration authorities for visa cancellation.
  • Cash-Only Enrolments: Demands for tuition fees to be paid in untraceable cash or to personal bank accounts rather than institutional treasury portals.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an international student study part-time in Australia on a student visa?

No. Under Student Visa Condition 8202, international students must maintain full-time enrolment in a registered CRICOS course during compulsory academic study periods. Part-time study is only permissible under formal medical intervention or compassionate grounds formally approved in writing by the university.

What is the Tuition Protection Service (TPS)?

The Tuition Protection Service (TPS) is an Australian statutory initiative that protects international students in the rare event that an education provider defaults (closes or ceases delivering a registered course). The TPS ensures students can either complete their studies in an equivalent registered replacement course or receive a full refund of unspent tuition fees.

How can I verify if an education agent is authorized to recruit for an Australian university?

Australian universities are legally mandated under the ESOS framework to maintain a publicly accessible "Education Agent List" on their official websites. Cross-reference your consultant against the university's approved agent directory before authorizing representation.
