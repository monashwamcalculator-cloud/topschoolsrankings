Top 50 Schools in Victoria: 2025 VCE Rankings

Victoria’s 50 highest-performing schools ranked by 2025 VCE results — study scores of 40 and above as a share of all scores — with selective and independent schools clearly labelled, the full table, and parent FAQs.

Tables, images and URL checked 13 November 2026 · Verify time-sensitive facts

Victoria's VCE results produce the most argued-over school ranking in the state, and the argument usually starts with a misunderstanding: the headline figure is not an ATAR and not a pass rate. It is the share of a school's VCE study scores that reached 40 or above, out of 50 — the band the VCAA's scaling puts at roughly the top 9 per cent of each subject's cohort statewide. Ranked on that measure for the 2025 VCE (results released on 11 December 2025, with more than 65,000 students completing the VCE), the strongest full-cohort school in Victoria is a regional one: Ballarat Clarendon College, at 35.0 per cent, ahead of the selective Mac.Robertson Girls' High School (34.7) and Nossal High School (32.4). Forty-nine of the top 50 schools are in Greater Melbourne. Every figure below comes from the VCAA's official Senior Secondary Completion and Achievement Information workbook, cross-checked across the publishers who rank it — where they disagree, this page says so.

One thing this ranking does differently, and it matters: it ranks schools that actually had a Year 12 class in 2025. In the raw VCAA table, the top two places belong to Clyde Secondary College (44.4 per cent) and Cranbourne West Secondary College (40.5 per cent) — two fine new schools that had no Year 12 students at all in 2025. Clyde's figure comes from 21 Year 10 students who sat a single Unit 3–4 subject early. Presenting either as "Victoria's best school" would mislead you, so — like Better Education's ATAR-based ranking, which omits both — this table sets them and one other new-campus row aside, names them openly in the methodology note below, and ranks the 50 strongest full VCE cohorts in the state.

How this ranking works — and the three schools set aside

Each VCE study is scored on a scaled 0–50 scale with a state mean of 30. A score of 40 or above puts a student in roughly the top 9 per cent of that subject statewide. The ranking metric is therefore the percentage of all study scores a school's students earned that were 40+ — a share of scores, not of students, since each student contributes several scores. Alongside it we print each school's median study score and its Unit 3–4 student count exactly as the VCAA defines it: students enrolled in at least one Unit 3–4 subject, including accelerated Year 10s and 11s. It is not a Year 12 class size, and small counts swing widely year to year — treat a high percentage on a tiny cohort (Yeshivah College, 58 students, is the smallest cohort in this top 50) with more caution than the same figure on 600 students. The VCAA publishes the underlying workbook to describe school outcomes; it does not publish a league table — every ranking, including this one, is a publisher's ordering of that file, and ours was cross-checked line by line across Matrix Education, Better Education and SchoolExplorer, which agree on every percentage and median used here.

The three schools set aside. In the raw workbook order, Clyde Secondary College (44.4%, median 39) and Cranbourne West Secondary College (40.5%, median 39) sit first and second, and Bacchus Marsh Grammar's new Aintree (Woodlea) campus (33.3%, median 36) sits fifth. None of the three had a Year 12 cohort in 2025: Clyde's own website confirms its 21 counted students were Year 10s who sat one subject, General Mathematics; Cranbourne West's first Year 12 class is still to come; and the Woodlea campus had only reached Year 11 (its main Maddingley campus ranks separately, well down the table). Their students' scores are real and impressive — a school whose Year 10s average 39.7 in a Unit 3–4 subject is doing something right — but a ranking of VCE outcomes should compare like with like. If any of the three is on your shortlist, judge it on its junior results and growth, not on this table.

A closer look at the top five

Ballarat Clarendon College — Ballarat

The state's strongest full VCE cohort in 2025 is not in Melbourne at all. Ballarat Clarendon College — co-educational, Early Learning to Year 12, on Sturt Street in Ballarat — put 35.0 per cent of its study scores at 40 or above with a median of 37, from a substantial cohort of 296 Unit 3–4 students, so the figure is no small-sample accident. Two features make it a distinctive #1: it offers boarding for Years 7–12, which puts it on the map for regional families statewide, and its own published policies describe open entry with no entry tests — the top result in Victoria from a school that does not select its students by examination.

Mac.Robertson Girls' High School — Melbourne (Albert Park)

"MacRob" is the girls' half of Victoria's selective-entry system: a government school for academically high-achieving students in Years 9–12, at 350–370 Kings Way by Albert Park, and — in its own words — one of only four selective-entry high schools in Victoria and the only single-gender one. Entry is by selective examination (ACER for Year 9, EduTest for Years 10–11). Its 34.7 per cent at a median of 37 came from a cohort of 571 Unit 3–4 students — a scale that makes the consistency, not just the peak, the story.

Nossal High School — Berwick

Nossal, in Berwick on the former Monash University Berwick campus grounds (with a Monash partnership to match), is the co-educational academically selective government high school of Melbourne's south-east: Years 9–12 only, roughly 200 students per year level, entry by examination. It recorded 32.4 per cent of scores at 40+ with a median of 37 from 410 Unit 3–4 students — the strongest co-educational government result in the state, achieved in Melbourne's outer south-east rather than the inner-city postcodes that dominate the rest of this table.

Haileybury Girls College — Keysborough

Haileybury appears twice in this top 50 — Girls College at #4 (31.2%, median 36, 420 students) and the College at #12 — because the VCAA reports its parallel education model as two rows: boys and girls learn in separate single-gender schools on shared campuses from Year 5. The wider school is one of Australia's largest: founded in 1892, it reports enrolments exceeding 7,000 across campuses in Keysborough, Brighton, Berwick, Melbourne City, Darwin and online. The Girls College figure is the stronger of the two Keysborough rows in 2025.

Ruyton Girls' School — Kew

Ruyton rounds out the top five at 30.9 per cent (median 36) from a deliberately small cohort of 163 Unit 3–4 students in Kew. It heads a dense Kew–Canterbury–Balwyn cluster of girls' schools — Fintona, Camberwell Girls', Methodist Ladies' College, Genazzano and Strathcona all sit inside the top 40 — that forms the geographic centre of gravity of this entire ranking. Small-cohort caution applies in principle, but Ruyton's median of 36 shows the strength runs through the cohort, not just its top students.

What the table tells you

The independent sector owns this list — but the exceptions are the interesting part. Forty of the top 50 are independent schools and four are Catholic; only six are government schools. Yet five of those six are selective or specialist — MacRob, Nossal, Melbourne High (#7), Suzanne Cory (#14) and John Monash Science School (#27) — and the sixth, Balwyn High (#42), is a large zoned school whose 19.2 per cent from 622 Unit 3–4 students is arguably a harder achievement than several smaller schools above it. Victoria's pattern is consistent: at the top of the VCE, you are either paying fees, passing a selective exam, or attending one of a handful of exceptional zoned schools.

Girls' schools are disproportionately strong. Twenty of the top 50 are girls' schools (against eight boys' schools and 22 co-educational), and they hold three of the top five places and five of the top ten. The Kew–Canterbury–Balwyn corridor alone contributes a run of them. Whatever the cause — cohort effects, subject mix, school culture — a family shortlisting on results alone will keep landing on girls' schools in Melbourne's east. And only one regional school makes the 50: Ballarat Clarendon, at #1. The next regional performers (Shepparton's Goulburn Valley Grammar and Bendigo's Girton Grammar) sit just outside, so regional families reading this table should treat it as a Melbourne list with one outstanding exception, and weigh boarding (Clarendon's own offering among them) or local results beyond the 40+ band.

Using this ranking well, step by step

  1. Start with fit, not rank: sector, single-sex or co-ed, faith, and your suburb's travel reality will cut this list to a realistic five faster than any percentage gap.
  2. Read the percentage with its cohort: a 21% from 400 students is a different claim from 21% from 90 — check the Unit 3–4 students column before ranking two schools against each other.
  3. For selective schools (MacRob, Melbourne High, Nossal, Suzanne Cory), treat entry as its own project: one examination decides it, so preparation and a fallback zoned option both belong in the plan.
  4. Visit before you conclude: open days and school tours show you the cohort your child would actually join — the median study score (the middle student's result) is the table's best proxy for that, and it is printed for every school.
  5. Re-check next December: VCE tables refresh annually, and single-year movement of a few places is normal noise, especially for small cohorts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which school topped Victoria's 2025 VCE results?

Among schools with a full Year 12 cohort, Ballarat Clarendon College — 35.0 per cent of its VCE study scores were 40 or above, with a median study score of 37. In the raw VCAA table, two new schools without a Year 12 class in 2025 (Clyde Secondary College, 44.4%, and Cranbourne West Secondary College, 40.5%) sit higher on the strength of junior students sitting single subjects early; this ranking sets them aside, explains why in its methodology note, and ranks full cohorts — the same convention Better Education's ATAR ranking uses.

What does "percentage of study scores of 40+" actually mean?

Every VCE study is scored on a scaled 0–50 scale (state mean 30). A study score of 40 or above places a student in roughly the top 9 per cent of that subject's cohort across Victoria. The figure in this table is the share of all study scores earned by a school's students that reached 40+ — not the share of students, since each student earns several scores. The median study score, also shown, is the middle score across the same set and is the better guide to the typical student's result.

How do Melbourne's selective schools perform?

Very strongly — they are the government sector's presence at the top of the table. Mac.Robertson Girls' ranks #2 (34.7%), Nossal #3 (32.4%), Melbourne High #7 (29.9%) and Suzanne Cory #14 (25.0%), with the specialist John Monash Science School at #27 (22.0%). All select by examination for Year 9 entry (with smaller intakes later), so their results reflect a selected cohort — compare them with each other, and with open-entry schools, on that understanding.

Why is only one regional school in the top 50?

Because the 40+ measure concentrates in Melbourne's east and in selective intakes — and because Ballarat Clarendon College (#1, 35.0%) is a genuine outlier, a regional school outperforming every metropolitan one. The next regional schools, Goulburn Valley Grammar (Shepparton) and Girton Grammar (Bendigo), fall just outside the 50. Regional families should read beyond this single metric: median scores, subject breadth and boarding options (Clarendon boards from Year 7) all matter, and several strong regional schools sit in the band immediately below this table.

Does a higher rank mean a school is better for my child?

Not on its own. This table measures one thing — the top band of VCE study scores in 2025 — for cohorts of very different sizes and intakes: selective schools examine their students in, fee-paying schools select by capacity to pay, and open-entry schools like Ballarat Clarendon and Balwyn High teach whoever enrols. Small cohorts swing year to year, and the table says nothing about wellbeing, teaching quality in the middle bands, or fit. Use it to build a shortlist, then visit: our guide to comparing schools beyond headline results walks through the questions that actually separate them.
