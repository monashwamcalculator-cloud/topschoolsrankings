University applications involve dozens of steps spread across 12–18 months — from standardized tests and school research to essays, recommendations, and financial aid forms. Missing a single deadline can mean waiting an entire year. This planner keeps you organized and on track.

This Application Timeline Planner generates a month-by-month checklist based on your target country's admissions system (USA, UK/UCAS, Australia, or Canada). It covers key milestones from test preparation through to enrollment.

How to use this planner

  • Select your application system — different countries have different timelines and processes.
  • Indicate if you're international — international students have additional visa and credential steps.
  • Click "Show timeline" for a personalized month-by-month checklist.

Select your application system for an illustrative timeline checklist.

Understanding your results

The timeline shows key milestones in chronological order. USA applications typically run from spring of junior year through January of senior year. UK UCAS applications are due by January 15 (October 15 for Oxbridge/medicine). Australian applications have different timelines for Semester 1 (February start) and Semester 2 (July start).

Start early — the biggest mistake students make is underestimating how long essays, recommendations, and financial aid applications take.

Frequently asked questions

The Common Application opens on August 1. Early Decision/Early Action deadlines are typically November 1–15. Regular Decision deadlines are usually January 1–15. Results come out in December (early) or March–April (regular).

The main UCAS deadline is January 15 for most courses. Oxford, Cambridge, medicine, dentistry, and veterinary science have an earlier deadline of October 15. Late applications are accepted until June 30 but may have fewer available places.

Ideally, start 12–18 months before your target enrollment. For US fall entry, start in the spring of your junior year (around March–April). For UK September entry via UCAS, start at the beginning of Year 12 (September–October of the prior year).

How to interpret the result

Use the result to compare scenarios and identify questions. Schools, universities, testing bodies and government agencies may use different scales, rounding rules and eligibility criteria. An estimate on this page cannot override an official calculation.

Before you decide

  • Read the assumptions shown beside the tool.
  • Repeat the calculation with a lower and higher scenario.
  • Save the official requirement that applies to your case.
  • Ask the responsible institution to confirm any conversion in writing.
