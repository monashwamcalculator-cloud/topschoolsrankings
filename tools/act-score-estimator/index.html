The ACT is an alternative to the SAT for US college admissions. It tests four sections — English, Math, Reading, and Science — each scored 1–36. Your composite score is the average of these four sections. Understanding your composite and where it ranks helps with college targeting and scholarship applications.

This ACT Score Estimator calculates your composite score from individual section scores and shows your competitiveness band for college admissions.

How to use this estimator

  • Enter each section score — English, Math, Reading, and Science (1–36 each).
  • Click "Estimate composite" to see your average and competitiveness band.

Enter ACT section scores (1–36 each) for composite and band.

Understanding your results

The national average ACT composite is approximately 20. A score of 25+ is above the 75th percentile. 30+ is competitive for top-50 universities, and 33+ is competitive for Ivy League-caliber schools. The ACT composite is simply the average of your four section scores, rounded to the nearest whole number.

If one section is pulling your composite down, targeted prep in that area can boost your overall score significantly.

Frequently asked questions

Many merit scholarships start at ACT 28–30. Full-tuition scholarships at state universities often require 32+. At community colleges and regional universities, a score of 25+ may qualify you for substantial aid.

Neither test is inherently easier — they test different skills in different formats. The ACT has more questions in less time and includes a science section. The SAT emphasizes analytical reasoning. Take practice tests for both to see which you prefer.

How to interpret the result

Use the result to compare scenarios and identify questions. Schools, universities, testing bodies and government agencies may use different scales, rounding rules and eligibility criteria. An estimate on this page cannot override an official calculation.

Before you decide

  • Read the assumptions shown beside the tool.
  • Repeat the calculation with a lower and higher scenario.
  • Save the official requirement that applies to your case.
  • Ask the responsible institution to confirm any conversion in writing.
