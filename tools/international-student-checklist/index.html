International Student Application Checklist

Enter your figures or complete the checklist. Results are planning estimates, not official decisions.

Applying to university as an international student involves significantly more paperwork than domestic applications. Beyond academic transcripts and test scores, you'll need credential evaluations, visa documentation, financial statements, and health records. Missing even one document can delay your application or visa.

This International Student Checklist generates a personalized document and preparation checklist based on your academic level, English test status, and visa requirements. It helps ensure you don't overlook any critical steps.

How to use this checklist

  • Select your level — undergraduate or graduate applications have different requirements.
  • Indicate English test status — this determines if test preparation needs to be included.
  • Indicate visa requirement — visa-requiring students need additional financial and health documentation.

Answer a few questions for a personalized document checklist .

Understanding your results

The checklist shows documents and steps organized by priority. Start with items that take the longest to obtain — credential evaluations can take 4–8 weeks, and visa processing can take 2–4 months in some countries. Financial documentation (bank statements, sponsor letters) should show at least 12 months of tuition and living costs.

Frequently asked questions

Common requirements include: academic transcripts (often officially translated), standardized test scores (SAT/ACT, GRE), English proficiency scores (IELTS/TOEFL), credential evaluation report (WES/ECE), passport, financial documentation (bank statements, sponsor letters), health records, and visa application forms.

You typically need to prove you can cover one year of tuition plus living expenses. For the USA (F-1 visa), this means showing $50,000–$80,000+. For the UK (Student visa), around £25,000–£40,000. Requirements vary by country and institution — check your I-20 (USA) or CAS (UK) for the exact amount.

How to interpret the result

Use the result to compare scenarios and identify questions. Schools, universities, testing bodies and government agencies may use different scales, rounding rules and eligibility criteria. An estimate on this page cannot override an official calculation.

Before you decide

  • Read the assumptions shown beside the tool.
  • Repeat the calculation with a lower and higher scenario.
  • Save the official requirement that applies to your case.
  • Ask the responsible institution to confirm any conversion in writing.
