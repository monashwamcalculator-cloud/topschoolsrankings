Applying to university as an international student involves significantly more paperwork than a domestic application. Beyond transcripts and test scores, you may need financial evidence, a visa application and destination-specific documents — and the exact list depends on your level, your destination and how far along you are.

That is exactly what the tool above does: answer the four questions — academic level, destination country (USA, UK, Canada or Australia), English test status and application stage — and it builds a personalised document and preparation checklist from your answers. Tick items off as you complete them; your progress is saved in this browser so you can return later, and you can print the list or save it as a PDF.

How to use this checklist

  • Select your academic level — school, undergraduate and postgraduate applicants are asked for different documents. Postgraduate applicants, for example, usually need a degree certificate and two references.
  • Select your destination country — this adds that country's visa route, such as the Form I-20 process for the USA, the CAS for the UK, the DLI check and study permit for Canada, or the CoE and subclass 500 visa for Australia.
  • Select your English test status — if you have not taken a test yet, the list includes choosing and booking one; if you already have a score, it includes checking the score against each university's overall and per-section minimums.
  • Select your application stage — researching, applying, holding an offer, or at the visa stage. Later stages add their own steps: the visa stage, for example, adds the specific visa documents for your destination.

Changing any answer rebuilds the list immediately. Ticks on items that are still on your new list are kept, and your answers and ticks are stored only in this browser — nothing is sent to us or to anyone else.

Understanding your checklist

The list is organised in sections: documents everyone needs, items for your level, English test steps, destination-specific items, and the steps for your current stage. Start with the items that depend on other people or offices — references, any credential evaluation your university requires, and visa steps — because their timelines are outside your control. Treat the list as a planning aid, not a ruling: always confirm each item against your university's own instructions and the official government immigration website for your destination before relying on it.

Frequently asked questions

What documents do most international applicants need? Common requirements include a valid passport, academic transcripts (officially translated if they are not in English), English proficiency scores (IELTS, TOEFL, PTE Academic or Duolingo English Test, where required), references, a personal statement or statement of purpose, and financial evidence. The personalised list above shows the set that applies to your answers.

How much money do I need to show? You generally need to evidence tuition fees plus living costs for the period your visa rules specify. The exact amount is set by your university and destination — for example, it is stated on the Form I-20 (USA), tied to your CAS (UK), or set out in the official visa guidance for Canada and Australia — so use the figure in your own university and visa documents rather than a general estimate.

Does this checklist guarantee admission or a visa? No. It is a planning tool built from standard, publicly documented steps. Only your university and the relevant government authority can confirm what applies to you, and their requirements override anything on this page.

Before you rely on this list

  • Keep copies of everything you submit, with dates.
  • If anything here disagrees with an official source, follow the official source.
