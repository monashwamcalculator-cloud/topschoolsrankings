If you're studying at an Australian university like Monash, Melbourne, or UNSW, your academic performance is measured by a Weighted Average Mark (WAM) out of 100. However, if you're applying for international graduate schools, MBA programs, or US-based jobs, you'll almost always be asked to provide a GPA on a 4.0 scale.

How do you convert WAM to GPA?

There is no single "official" conversion rate because the Australian and US grading systems evaluate students differently. In the US, scoring above 90% is common and expected for an 'A' grade. In Australia, achieving above 80% (High Distinction) is extremely difficult and is considered equivalent to a perfect 4.0 A grade internationally.

Standard Conversion Scale

Our calculator uses a widely used approximate mapping based on common Australian university grade bands. There is no single official WAM-to-GPA standard — universities and credential evaluators convert transcripts individually — so treat this scale as a planning estimate:

  • WAM 80 - 100 (High Distinction): 4.0 GPA (A)
  • WAM 70 - 79 (Distinction): 3.3 - 3.9 GPA (B+ to A-)
  • WAM 60 - 69 (Credit): 2.7 - 3.2 GPA (B- to B)
  • WAM 50 - 59 (Pass): 2.0 - 2.6 GPA (C)
  • WAM < 50 (Fail): 0.0 GPA (F)

Frequently asked questions

Will graduate schools recalculate my WAM?

Yes. If you apply to a competitive US graduate school (like an Ivy League or a top MBA program), they will usually use a credential evaluation service like WES (World Education Services) to officially convert your Australian transcript into a US GPA. Our calculator provides a rough planning estimate only. An official evaluation may differ, because evaluators such as WES convert letter grades rather than WAM percentage bands.

Why the same WAM can convert to different GPAs

This converter maps your WAM through Australian grade bands — High Distinction, Distinction, Credit, Pass — because that is how a percentage average is usually read. Official credential evaluators work differently: services such as WES convert subject by subject, translating each unit's grade into the US letter system before averaging. Two students with an identical WAM of 74 can therefore receive slightly different official GPAs if one earned steady Distinctions while the other mixed High Distinctions with Credits, even though this calculator gives both the same planning estimate.

Failed and repeated units are another source of difference. Your WAM normally includes every attempted unit, fails included, in the denominator of credit points. An evaluator converting a transcript sees those same fails, but how a repeat attempt is counted — both attempts, the latest, or the best — varies by evaluator and by the receiving institution's policy, so an official conversion can land above or below a band-based estimate.

Treat the figure here as a shortlisting tool: it is accurate enough to judge whether a programme's stated GPA minimum is realistically within reach and to compare your record against published class profiles. For the application itself, report your WAM exactly as your Australian university prints it, state the scale (out of 100), and let the receiving institution or its evaluator perform the official conversion.
