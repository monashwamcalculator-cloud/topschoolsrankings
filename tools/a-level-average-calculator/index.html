A-Level Average Grade Calculator

Enter your figures or complete the checklist. Results are planning estimates, not official decisions.

A-Level results are central to UK university admissions, and understanding your average grade helps you target appropriate courses. Universities often specify offers in letter grades (e.g., AAB) rather than as a numerical average, but knowing your overall grade profile helps with planning.

This A-Level Average Grade Calculator takes your individual A-Level grades and calculates an average grade and UCAS-style summary. It's useful for understanding your overall academic profile.

How to use this calculator

  • Enter each A-Level grade — select from A* to E for up to four subjects.
  • Click "Calculate average" to see your average grade and UCAS-style profile.

Enter up to four A-Level grades to see average grade and UCAS-style summary.

Understanding your results

Your average grade is calculated by converting letter grades to numerical points (A*=6, A=5, B=4, etc.) and averaging. An average grade of A (5.0) across three subjects is very competitive. Most Russell Group universities require AAA or AAB equivalent for popular courses.

Frequently asked questions

Top Russell Group universities typically require A*A*A to AAB for competitive courses. Oxbridge often requires A*A*A or A*AA. Less competitive universities may accept BBB or BCC. Check each course's specific entry requirements on UCAS.

Most UK universities require three A-Levels. Some students take four in Year 12 and drop to three in Year 13. Taking four A-Levels to completion is only necessary for highly competitive courses at Oxbridge or medicine.

How to interpret the result

Use the result to compare scenarios and identify questions. Schools, universities, testing bodies and government agencies may use different scales, rounding rules and eligibility criteria. An estimate on this page cannot override an official calculation.

Before you decide

  • Read the assumptions shown beside the tool.
  • Repeat the calculation with a lower and higher scenario.
  • Save the official requirement that applies to your case.
  • Ask the responsible institution to confirm any conversion in writing.
