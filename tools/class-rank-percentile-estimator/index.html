Class Rank Percentile Calculator

Enter your figures or complete the checklist. Results are planning estimates, not official decisions.

Class rank shows where you stand academically compared to your classmates. US colleges often consider percentile rank — if you're ranked 5th in a class of 200, you're in the top 2.5%. Many schools are moving away from reporting exact rank, but if your school provides it, knowing your percentile helps with college applications.

This Class Rank Percentile Calculator converts your rank and class size into a percentile, showing the percentage of students you've outperformed.

How to use this calculator

  • Enter your rank — 1 means you're at the top of your class.
  • Enter your class size — the total number of students in your graduating class.

Calculate percentile from class rank .

Understanding your results

Percentile represents the percentage of students you rank above. Being in the top 10% (90th percentile or higher) is considered very strong for competitive college admissions. The top 25% is solid for most universities. Some colleges guarantee admission for students in the top 10% of their class (like Texas's automatic admission rule).

Frequently asked questions

Some do, many don't. About 50% of US high schools still report class rank. Colleges that consider rank use it alongside GPA and test scores. Many competitive high schools have stopped reporting rank because their students are clustered so closely together.

Ivy League admits typically come from the top 1–5% of their class, though schools without rank reporting can still be competitive. Class rank is just one factor — essays, activities, recommendations, and test scores all play important roles.

How to interpret the result

Use the result to compare scenarios and identify questions. Schools, universities, testing bodies and government agencies may use different scales, rounding rules and eligibility criteria. An estimate on this page cannot override an official calculation.

Before you decide

  • Read the assumptions shown beside the tool.
  • Repeat the calculation with a lower and higher scenario.
  • Save the official requirement that applies to your case.
  • Ask the responsible institution to confirm any conversion in writing.
