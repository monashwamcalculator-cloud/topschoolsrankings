NSW selective high schools are among the most competitive schools in Australia, with schools like James Ruse Agricultural High School achieving top HSC and ATAR results year after year. The selective school placement test assesses reading, mathematical reasoning, and thinking skills, and the competition for places is intense.

This NSW Selective School Score Estimator provides an illustrative readiness assessment based on practice test scores. It is not an official result — only NSW Education publishes actual placement outcomes — but it helps parents and students set realistic preparation goals.

How to use this estimator

  • Enter practice test scores — reading, mathematical reasoning, and thinking skills as percentages.
  • Optionally add writing quality — on a 1–10 scale from practice assessments.
  • Click "Estimate readiness" to see an illustrative readiness band.

Enter your child's practice test scores (or mock exam percentages) to get an illustrative readiness band for NSW selective high schools. For official rules see our NSW selective exam guide .

Understanding your results

High-demand schools like James Ruse typically require scores above 90% across all sections. Schools like Baulkham Hills, North Sydney, and Sydney Boys/Girls are slightly less competitive but still require strong performance (80–90%+). Regional selective schools may have lower thresholds. Start preparation 12–18 months before the test for best results.

Frequently asked questions

James Ruse is the most competitive selective school in NSW. Based on historical data, successful applicants typically score in the top 1–2% on the placement test, which roughly translates to 90–95%+ across all sections on practice tests. However, actual cutoffs vary each year.

Most education experts recommend starting preparation 12–18 months before the test. This allows time to build skills gradually rather than cramming. Regular practice tests, reading comprehension exercises, and mathematical reasoning training are key preparation strategies.

While many successful students attend coaching, it's not strictly necessary. Self-study with quality practice materials, regular reading, and strong school performance can be equally effective.
