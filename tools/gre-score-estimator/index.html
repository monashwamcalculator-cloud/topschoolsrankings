GRE Score Estimator (Verbal + Quant)

Enter your figures or complete the checklist. Results are planning estimates, not official decisions.

The GRE (Graduate Record Examinations) is required or recommended for many graduate school applications worldwide. It has two scored sections — Verbal Reasoning (130–170) and Quantitative Reasoning (130–170) — for a combined maximum of 340. Different graduate programs weight sections differently based on their discipline.

This GRE Score Estimator sums your verbal and quantitative scores and shows an illustrative competitiveness band for graduate school admissions.

How to use this estimator

  • Enter your Verbal score — 130 to 170.
  • Enter your Quantitative score — 130 to 170.
  • Click "Estimate total" to see your combined score and band.

Enter GRE Verbal and Quantitative scores (130–170 each).

Understanding your results

A combined score of 310+ is competitive for many master's programs. Top-20 programs typically expect 320+. For STEM fields, a strong quantitative score (165+) matters more, while humanities programs prioritize verbal scores (160+). Some programs are dropping GRE requirements — always check current admissions requirements.

Frequently asked questions

It varies widely by program. A combined 310+ is competitive for many programs. Top business schools expect 320+. Top engineering programs want 325+ with 165+ quantitative. Check each program's admitted student profiles for realistic targets.

How to interpret the result

Use the result to compare scenarios and identify questions. Schools, universities, testing bodies and government agencies may use different scales, rounding rules and eligibility criteria. An estimate on this page cannot override an official calculation.

Before you decide

  • Read the assumptions shown beside the tool.
  • Repeat the calculation with a lower and higher scenario.
  • Save the official requirement that applies to your case.
  • Ask the responsible institution to confirm any conversion in writing.
