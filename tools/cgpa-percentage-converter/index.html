CGPA to Percentage Converter (10-Point Scale)

Enter your figures or complete the checklist. Results are planning estimates, not official decisions.

Many Indian and South Asian universities use a 10-point CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average) scale instead of percentages. When applying for higher education abroad, you'll often need to convert your CGPA to a percentage or to a US 4.0-scale GPA.

This CGPA to Percentage Converter handles conversions between the 10-point CGPA scale and percentage grades in both directions. It uses the commonly accepted formula (CGPA × 9.5 = approximate percentage) as a starting point.

How to use this converter

  • Select conversion direction — CGPA to percentage, or percentage to CGPA.
  • Enter your value — for CGPA, enter 0–10; for percentage, enter 0–100.
  • Click "Convert" to see the result.

Understanding your results

The standard conversion multiplier (CGPA × 9.5) is widely used in India but some universities use different multipliers (×8.8, ×9.0, or custom formulas). Always check if your university publishes its own official conversion formula — many include it on your transcript or academic regulations page.

Frequently asked questions

For competitive programs in the USA, UK, or Canada, a CGPA of 8.0+ (76%+) is generally strong. Top universities often expect 8.5+ (80%+). For research-based master's and PhD programs, a CGPA of 7.5+ with strong research experience may be sufficient.

The CGPA × 9.5 formula is commonly used but not universal. CBSE boards use this formula, but individual universities may use different multipliers. Some use ×8.8 or ×10. Always use your institution's official conversion formula when available.

How to interpret the result

Use the result to compare scenarios and identify questions. Schools, universities, testing bodies and government agencies may use different scales, rounding rules and eligibility criteria. An estimate on this page cannot override an official calculation.

Before you decide

  • Read the assumptions shown beside the tool.
  • Repeat the calculation with a lower and higher scenario.
  • Save the official requirement that applies to your case.
  • Ask the responsible institution to confirm any conversion in writing.
