Sending a child to boarding school is a big decision that goes beyond academics and finances. A child's emotional maturity, independence level, and social adaptability all play crucial roles in determining whether boarding school will be a positive experience. This quiz helps parents assess readiness honestly.

This Boarding School Readiness Quiz asks about your child's age, independence level, and social adjustment to provide an illustrative readiness assessment. It's designed to spark honest conversation within the family, not to make the decision for you.

How to use this quiz

  • Select student age — younger children face different challenges than teenagers.
  • Rate independence level — can they manage daily routines without constant reminders?
  • Rate social adjustment — how well do they adapt to new environments and groups?

Answer honestly for an illustrative boarding school readiness assessment.

Understanding your results

The readiness assessment considers multiple factors. Children who manage their own schedules, homework, and personal care well tend to adapt faster to boarding school. Social adaptability is equally important — boarding school means living with peers 24/7. If readiness is "developing," consider starting with a short summer boarding program to build independence gradually.

Many boarding schools offer transition support, house parents, and counseling services specifically designed to help new students adjust. Talk to the school's pastoral care team about their support systems.

Frequently asked questions

The most common entry points are 11–13 (Year 7–9 in the UK, Grade 6–8 in the US). Some families choose sixth form entry (16+). Younger children benefit from the full boarding experience but need higher emotional readiness. There's no universally 'best' age — it depends on the individual child.

Start with short separations — overnight camps, week-long programs, and sleepovers at friends' houses. Teach daily living skills: laundry, time management, basic cooking, and budgeting pocket money. Discuss expectations openly and visit the school together before the first term.

Homesickness is normal and expected — most students experience it in the first few weeks. Good boarding schools have experienced house parents and pastoral care teams who help students through this transition. Regular (but not excessive) contact with home, involvement in school activities, and making friends all help. Most students adjust within 2–4 weeks.

How to interpret the result

Use the result to compare scenarios and identify questions. Schools, universities, testing bodies and government agencies may use different scales, rounding rules and eligibility criteria. An estimate on this page cannot override an official calculation.

Before you decide

  • Read the assumptions shown beside the tool.
  • Repeat the calculation with a lower and higher scenario.
  • Save the official requirement that applies to your case.
  • Ask the responsible institution to confirm any conversion in writing.
