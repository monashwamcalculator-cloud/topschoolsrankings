AP GPA Calculator (Weighted Estimate)

Enter your figures or complete the checklist. Results are planning estimates, not official decisions.

Advanced Placement (AP) courses carry extra weight in GPA calculations at most US high schools. While a regular A earns 4.0 points, an A in an AP course typically earns 5.0 points on a weighted scale. This weighted GPA reflects the additional difficulty of AP coursework and is valued by college admissions officers.

This AP GPA Calculator estimates your weighted GPA based on the number of AP/honors courses you're taking and your unweighted average. It helps you understand how AP courses boost your GPA for college applications.

How to use this calculator

  • Enter number of AP/honors courses — these receive additional weight.
  • Enter number of regular courses — these use the standard 4.0 scale.
  • Enter your unweighted GPA — your average on the standard 4.0 scale.

Estimate weighted GPA from AP vs regular courses.

Understanding your results

A weighted GPA above 4.0 indicates you're taking rigorous AP/honors courses and performing well. Most competitive colleges recalculate GPA using their own weighting systems, but seeing your weighted GPA helps you understand your academic standing relative to peers taking similar courses.

Quality matters more than quantity — admissions officers prefer strong grades in 4–6 AP courses over mediocre grades in 10+ AP courses.

Frequently asked questions

Most competitive colleges expect 4–8 AP courses over four years of high school. Ivy League applicants often take 7–12. However, the quality of your performance matters more than the number — a B in AP Biology is less impressive than an A in regular Biology to some admissions officers.

Weighted GPA shows course rigor, which is important. However, most colleges recalculate your GPA using their own system. They look at your transcript holistically — the courses you chose, your grades, and grade trends matter more than the raw weighted number.

How to interpret the result

Use the result to compare scenarios and identify questions. Schools, universities, testing bodies and government agencies may use different scales, rounding rules and eligibility criteria. An estimate on this page cannot override an official calculation.

Before you decide

  • Read the assumptions shown beside the tool.
  • Repeat the calculation with a lower and higher scenario.
  • Save the official requirement that applies to your case.
  • Ask the responsible institution to confirm any conversion in writing.
