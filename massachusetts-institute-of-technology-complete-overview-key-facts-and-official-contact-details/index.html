Basic profile

  • Full name: Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT).
  • Type: Private research university.
  • Location: 77 Massachusetts Ave, Cambridge, MA 02139, United States.
  • Campus: Midsize city, approximately 166 acres (about 67.2 hectares).
  • Founded: Chartered on April 10, 1861; widely recognized as established in 1861.
  • Global role: MIT has played a significant role in the development of many areas of modern technology and science.

Key facts table

History and mission

  • Founding purpose: MIT was created by William Barton Rogers to promote education that combined scientific theory with practical application, at a time when the United States was industrializing rapidly.
  • Early development: Classes began in Boston in 1865; the institute later moved across the Charles River to its current Cambridge campus, gradually expanding its science and engineering programs and adding architecture, management, humanities, and social sciences.
  • Mission: MIT’s mission is to advance knowledge and educate students in science, technology, and areas of scholarship that will best serve the nation and the world in the 21st century.

Academic structure

MIT’s academics are organized into five schools plus the Schwarzman College of Computing.

  • Degrees offered: MIT offers bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees, as well as professional degrees and joint programs across these schools.
  • Curriculum style: The undergraduate curriculum combines a strong General Institute Requirements core (math, science, humanities, arts, social sciences) with specialized majors, emphasizing laboratory work and project‑based learning.

Research and innovation

  • Research intensity: MIT is recognized as one of the world’s most research‑intensive universities, with annual research expenditures in the billions of dollars across government, industry, and foundation‑funded projects.
  • Major research areas: Key strengths include artificial intelligence, robotics, information technology, energy and climate, aerospace engineering, biotechnology, materials science, nanotechnology, and economics.
  • Institutes and labs: MIT hosts many major research units such as the Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (CSAIL), the Media Lab, the Koch Institute for Integrative Cancer Research, and the MIT Energy Initiative.
  • Innovation ecosystem: The institute supports entrepreneurship through programs like the Martin Trust Center for MIT Entrepreneurship and various accelerators, helping students and faculty launch startups that have a significant global economic impact.

Admissions and outcomes

  • Selectivity: Recent undergraduate acceptance rates are under 5%, with your screenshot showing 4.7% for 2023, reflecting extremely competitive admissions.
  • Evaluation: MIT looks for strong academic preparation in math and science, evidence of creativity and problem‑solving, research or project experience, and alignment with MIT’s mission to use science and technology to serve society.
  • Career results: Graduates frequently secure high‑impact roles in technology, engineering, finance, consulting, research, and entrepreneurship, with high average starting salaries and long‑term earning potential, as illustrated by the 1.43 lakh USD average salary figure in your screenshot (verify with MIT Career Advising & Professional Development data for the latest report).

Student life and campus culture

  • Residential system: Most undergraduates live in residence halls on or near campus, each with its own culture and traditions, while many graduate students live in MIT housing or nearby Cambridge and Boston neighborhoods.
  • Learning culture: The environment is known for being rigorous but collaborative, with students engaging in problem sets, lab work, and the Undergraduate Research Opportunities Program (UROP), which allows undergraduates to work directly on faculty research.
  • Activities: MIT offers numerous student organizations, athletics, performing arts groups, robotics teams, hackathons, and maker spaces, reflecting a culture that values creativity, hands‑on building, and innovation.
