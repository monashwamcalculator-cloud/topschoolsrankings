Where Is Columbia University in NYC? Address, Campuses and Directions (2026)

Columbia University is at 535 West 116th Street, New York, NY 10027, in Morningside Heights. 1 train to 116th St — campuses, directions, maps and visiting info.

Tables, images and URL checked 26 November 2026 · Verify time-sensitive facts
Quick answer

Columbia University's main campus is at 535 West 116th Street, New York, NY 10027, in Morningside Heights, Manhattan. Take the 1 train to 116th Street–Columbia University, right outside the main gates. The Manhattanville campus is nine blocks north, and the Medical Center (CUIMC) is at 168th Street in Washington Heights.

Editorial note: Campus borders, shuttle routes, and visitor policies can change. Always check the official Columbia University map and visitor guidelines before planning a trip to New York City.

Columbia University is one of the best-known universities in the world, and one of the hardest to place on a map if you have never been to New York. Its main campus is not in Midtown and not downtown — it is a walled quadrangle in Morningside Heights, on the Upper West Side of Manhattan, at 535 West 116th Street. The 1 train stops right outside its main gates. But that is only one of Columbia's campuses: the Business School and the neuroscience labs are nine blocks north in Manhattanville, and the doctors, dentists, nurses and public-health students study about 50 blocks further north, at 168th Street in Washington Heights.

This guide gives you the address, the neighbourhood and the route for every Columbia campus — Morningside Heights, Manhattanville, the Medical Center, the Baker Athletic Complex and the Lamont-Doherty Earth Observatory — plus how to visit, where students live, and how safe the area is. Every fact was checked on 26 November 2026 against Columbia University's own websites: the Visitors Center, Columbia Transportation, Columbia Housing and Columbia Public Safety.

The Morningside Heights Main Campus

When people refer to "Columbia University", they are almost always talking about the historic Morningside Heights campus. Columbia's own Visitors Center describes the university as located on the Upper West Side of Manhattan, in Morningside Heights. The campus is a single enclosed quadrangle, bounded by Broadway to the west, Amsterdam Avenue to the east, West 114th Street to the south and West 120th Street to the north — six city blocks that feel very different from the spread-out campuses of other New York universities, such as NYU in Greenwich Village.

The campus was designed by the firm McKim, Mead & White and dedicated in 1896, when Columbia moved here from Madison Avenue. Its centrepiece is Low Memorial Library, a domed building facing south across College Walk — which is simply 116th Street running through the campus — and the main gates stand at 116th Street and Broadway. The Visitors Center is inside Low Library, in Room 213.

Morningside Heights is home to the three undergraduate schools — Columbia College, the Fu Foundation School of Engineering and Applied Science, and the School of General Studies — and to graduate and professional schools for arts and sciences, architecture, the arts, international affairs, journalism, law and social work. The School of Professional Studies is also here. The Business School used to be on this campus too, but it moved to Manhattanville in January 2022, so it no longer belongs in this list.

The Manhattanville Campus

Just nine blocks north of Morningside Heights lies Columbia's second Manhattan campus, in West Harlem. The 17-acre Manhattanville campus runs from West 125th Street to West 133rd Street, between Broadway and 12th Avenue, and it is master-planned by the Renzo Piano Building Workshop as an open campus embedded in the street grid — no gates, no walls, with ground floors largely open to the public.

Manhattanville is where the university's newest buildings stand. The Jerome L. Greene Science Center, at 3227 Broadway, opened in 2017 and houses the Zuckerman Mind Brain Behavior Institute's neuroscience researchers in a 450,000-square-foot building. Beside it are the Lenfest Center for the Arts (615 West 129th Street, also 2017) and The Forum, a conference centre opened in 2018. In January 2022 the Columbia Business School opened its new home here: Henry R. Kravis Hall and David Geffen Hall, two buildings totalling about 492,000 square feet, facing each other across The Square, a one-acre landscaped space that is the largest public park on any Columbia campus. The long-term plan for Manhattanville will eventually add 6.8 million square feet of academic space.

For a visitor, the practical difference is the feel. Morningside is a walled, historic quadrangle; Manhattanville is glass, open lawns and public paths. The two are about half a mile apart — a 10-minute walk up Broadway, or a ride on the free Manhattanville Loop shuttle.

Columbia University Irving Medical Center (CUIMC)

Students in medicine, dentistry, nursing or public health do not study in Morningside Heights at all. They are based at the Columbia University Irving Medical Center, about 50 blocks north of the main campus at 168th Street and Broadway, in Washington Heights. The medical campus is shared with NewYork-Presbyterian Hospital — the two institutions named their joint campus for Herbert and Florence Irving in 2016 — and it is home to the Vagelos College of Physicians and Surgeons, the dental, nursing and public-health schools, and the hospital itself.

CUIMC's address is 630 West 168th Street, New York, NY 10032. It is reached on the A, C or 1 train to 168th Street, or on the M2, M3, M4, M5 or M100 buses. On weekdays the free Intercampus Shuttle also runs between Morningside Heights, Manhattanville and CUIMC.

Getting to Columbia: Subway, Bus and Shuttles

The simplest way to reach Columbia is the subway, and the 1 train does almost all the work. The 1 train stops at 116th Street–Columbia University, directly outside the main gates; at 125th Street for Manhattanville; and at 168th Street for the Medical Center (where the A and C also stop). Buses M4, M5, M11, M60 and M104 serve the Morningside Heights neighbourhood, and the M2, M3, M4, M5 and M100 buses serve CUIMC. New York's subway runs 24 hours a day, seven days a week.

Two things are worth checking before you travel. First, the MTA has been carrying out rehabilitation work at the 125th Street station on the 1 line since October 2026, which can affect service to Manhattanville; Columbia added a Saturday shuttle looping every 10 to 15 minutes between the 125th Street and 137th Street subway stops while the work continues. Second, Columbia's own shuttles are free for students, faculty and staff — but they are not all one service. The Intercampus Shuttle runs Monday to Friday between Morningside Heights, Manhattanville, CUIMC and Harlem Hospital; the Manhattanville Loop Shuttle runs between the two lower campuses; the Lamont Shuttle runs Monday to Friday (with limited Saturday service) out to the Lamont-Doherty Earth Observatory in Palisades, New York; and Public Safety's Evening Shuttle runs from 4 p.m. into the early morning. Shuttle locations can be tracked in real time with the TripShot app.

Arriving from further away, trains come into Grand Central or Penn Station and buses into the Port Authority Bus Terminal, from where it is a subway ride or a taxi north to campus. By car, the usual route is the Henry Hudson Parkway to the 95th or 96th Street exit. There is no visitor parking on campus, so public transport is the practical choice.

Visiting Columbia

The Visitors Center is in Room 213 of Low Library, at the centre of the Morningside campus. It is open Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., except on University holidays, and all of its tours are free. Guided historical tours meet there; at weekends, groups are free to explore the campus on their own using the self-guided tour.

Columbia's outdoor areas — the plazas, walkways and lawns of Morningside Heights and Manhattanville — are open to visitors, and you are welcome to explore them at your leisure. Academic buildings, libraries and dormitories are a different matter: a current Columbia ID is required to enter them, with St. Paul's Chapel the one exception. Gate access is controlled and the rules can change, so check the Visitors Center's current guidance before you go, and call 212-854-4900 with any questions. Photography and filming are allowed in outdoor spaces, including College Walk along 116th Street between Broadway and Amsterdam Avenue.

If you are staying overnight, there are a number of hotels close to campus on the Upper West Side, and Columbia has negotiated rates at several of them for visitors — the details are on the university's travel portal.

Living on Campus: Housing in Morningside Heights

First-year students at Columbia College and the School of Engineering and Applied Science are required to live on campus, and they are guaranteed housing for up to four years — eight terms — provided they maintain eligibility. The guarantee extends to all Columbia College and Columbia Engineering undergraduates who apply to live on campus and remain continuously registered as full-time students (second-year Combined Plan students and visiting students are the exceptions). Each spring, students choose their rooms for the following year through Room Selection, a lottery that takes seniority into account.

First-years live in one of five residence halls: Carman Hall, Furnald Hall, John Jay Hall, Wallach Hall or Wien Hall. The only way out of the first-year live-on rule is approval from Residential Life to commute from a parent or guardian's home; first-years are also barred from fraternity or sorority housing. School of General Studies students are not eligible for undergraduate housing. Because the guarantee covers the whole degree, students can spend all four years in Columbia housing on or immediately around the Morningside campus, rather than in the private rental market.

Safety and the Surrounding Neighborhood

Morningside Heights is a residential, student-heavy neighbourhood of restaurants, coffee shops, bookstores, theatres and parks, and the northwest entrance to Central Park is just under a mile from campus. The university's own Public Safety department patrols the Morningside, Manhattanville and Medical Center campuses 24 hours a day, seven days a week, with walking and vehicle patrols and guard booths at the campus entrances, most of them staffed around the clock.

The practical resources for getting around safely are the free Evening Shuttle, which runs from 4 p.m. into the early morning for Columbia ID holders; walking safety escorts, available from 7 p.m. to 3 a.m.; Safe Haven businesses, marked with a red lion sticker in the window, where anyone in the Columbia community can ask for help; Blue Light emergency call boxes; and the LionSafe app, which carries emergency services, campus maps, shuttle schedules and crime alerts. The emergency numbers are 212-854-5555 for Morningside, 212-853-3333 for Manhattanville and 212-305-7979 for CUIMC; off campus, call 911. As in any part of New York, stay aware after dark and use the shuttle or an escort late at night rather than walking alone.

Exploring New York City from Columbia

One of the real advantages of Columbia's location is how much of the city is within reach of a student budget. Through the Arts Initiative, students get free admission to more than 20 New York museums — including the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the Museum of Modern Art, the Jewish Museum and El Museo del Barrio — and discounted tickets to hundreds of events, including Broadway and off-Broadway shows, Carnegie Hall, the Metropolitan Opera, the New York Philharmonic and New York City Ballet. Free tickets to performances are also given away through the Arts Initiative's email newsletter, and the Ticket and Information Center in Lerner Hall sells the discounted tickets.

The 1 train runs straight down Broadway to Midtown Manhattan and runs all night, so the city is genuinely usable as an extension of the campus — museums, theatres and concerts, not just sightseeing. That is the part of Columbia's location that no address can capture: the university sits inside the city, rather than beside it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Columbia University's address?

Columbia University's main (Morningside Heights) campus is at 535 West 116th Street, New York, NY 10027. The Manhattanville campus is at 3227 Broadway, New York, NY 10027, and the Medical Center (CUIMC) is at 630 West 168th Street, New York, NY 10032.

Which subway goes to Columbia University?

Take the 1 train to 116th Street–Columbia University for the Morningside Heights campus — the station is directly outside the main gates at Broadway and 116th Street. For Manhattanville, take the 1 train to 125th Street; for the Medical Center, take the A, C or 1 train to 168th Street.

Is Columbia University an open campus?

Yes, the outdoor plazas, walkways and green spaces of the Morningside Heights and Manhattanville campuses are open to visitors, and you can explore them at your leisure. Academic buildings, libraries and dormitories require a current Columbia ID — St. Paul's Chapel is the exception. Gate access is controlled and can change, so check the Visitors Center's current guidance before you go.

How far apart are the Morningside and Manhattanville campuses?

About nine blocks — roughly half a mile, or a 10-minute walk from the main gates at 116th Street and Broadway to the heart of Manhattanville at 125th Street. The free Manhattanville Loop shuttle also runs between the two campuses.

Does Columbia guarantee housing in NYC?

Yes. Columbia Housing guarantees housing for all Columbia College and Columbia Engineering undergraduates who apply to live on campus and remain continuously registered as full-time students. First-year students are required to live on campus and are guaranteed housing for up to four years (eight terms), provided they maintain eligibility.

Is Morningside Heights a safe neighborhood for students?

Morningside Heights is a residential, student-heavy neighborhood, and Columbia's Public Safety department patrols the Morningside, Manhattanville and Medical Center campuses 24 hours a day, seven days a week, with guard booths at the campus entrances. Students can also use the free Evening Shuttle from 4 p.m., walking safety escorts from 7 p.m. to 3 a.m., Safe Haven businesses and Blue Light emergency call boxes. As with anywhere in New York, stay aware after dark and use the shuttle or an escort late at night.

Do Columbia students have time to explore New York City?

Yes. Through the Arts Initiative, students get free admission to more than 20 New York museums, including the Met and MoMA, and discounted tickets to Broadway and off-Broadway shows, concerts and performances. The 1 train runs 24 hours a day, and Central Park is just under a mile from campus.

Are freshmen required to live on campus?

Yes. All first-year students in Columbia College and the School of Engineering and Applied Science are required to live on campus, unless Residential Life approves a commute from a parent or guardian's home. They live in one of five first-year residence halls — Carman, Furnald, John Jay, Wallach or Wien.
