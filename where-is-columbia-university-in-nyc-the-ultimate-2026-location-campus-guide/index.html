Where Is Columbia University in NYC? The Comprehensive 2026 Location & Campus Guide

If you are asking, "Where is Columbia University in NYC?", you are looking for more than just an address.

Tables, images and URL checked 20 August 2026 · Verify time-sensitive facts
Quick answer

Columbia University's main Morningside Heights campus is located in Upper Manhattan, New York City, bordered by Broadway, Amsterdam Avenue, West 114th Street, and West 120th Street. The separate Manhattanville campus is nearby, and the Medical Center is further north in Washington Heights.

Editorial note: Campus borders, shuttle routes, and visitor policies can change. Always check the official Columbia University map and visitor guidelines before planning a trip to New York City.

The Morningside Heights Main Campus

When people refer to "Columbia University," they are almost always talking about the historic Morningside Heights campus. Located in Upper Manhattan, this tightly integrated, 32-acre campus was designed by the famous architectural firm McKim, Mead & White in the late 19th century.

Unlike many urban universities that are fragmented and blended into the city streets (like NYU), Columbia features a distinct, traditional, closed-campus feel while still being located in the heart of one of the world's most vibrant cities. The main gates are located on Broadway and 116th Street.

Key Boundaries: The campus spans from West 114th Street in the south to West 120th Street in the north, and from Broadway in the west to Amsterdam Avenue in the east. It houses the undergraduate Columbia College, the Fu Foundation School of Engineering and Applied Science, and several major graduate schools including Law, Business, and Journalism.

The Manhattanville Campus Expansion

Just a few blocks north of the historic main campus lies Columbia's massive modern expansion in the Manhattanville neighborhood. This state-of-the-art, 17-acre development stretches roughly from West 125th Street to West 133rd Street.

Manhattanville is the new home of the Columbia Business School (David Geffen Hall and Henry R. Kravis Hall), the Jerome L. Greene Science Center, and the Lenfest Center for the Arts. This campus represents Columbia's modern future, featuring striking glass architecture and open public spaces designed to integrate seamlessly with the surrounding West Harlem community.

Columbia University Irving Medical Center (CUIMC)

Students studying medicine, nursing, dentistry, or public health will spend their time at the Columbia University Irving Medical Center. This massive medical and research complex is located significantly further north in the Washington Heights neighborhood, approximately 50 blocks north of the Morningside campus (around West 168th Street).

CUIMC is co-located with NewYork-Presbyterian Hospital, providing students with unparalleled access to clinical training and world-class medical research facilities.

Getting Around: Transport and Shuttles

Navigating between Columbia's campuses and the rest of New York City is highly efficient. The university relies heavily on the MTA (Metropolitan Transportation Authority) subway system.

  • To Morningside Heights: Take the 1 train to the 116th Street–Columbia University station, which stops directly outside the main gates.
  • To Manhattanville: Take the 1 train to the 125th Street station.
  • To the Medical Center: Take the 1, A, or C trains to the 168th Street station.

Additionally, Columbia operates a free Intercampus Shuttle system for students, faculty, and staff, running continuously between Morningside Heights, Manhattanville, CUIMC, and the Lamont-Doherty Earth Observatory in Palisades, NY.

Safety and the Surrounding Neighborhood

Morningside Heights is a bustling, residential neighborhood filled with students, academics, cafes, and bookstores. It is generally considered very safe, heavily patrolled by both the NYPD and Columbia University Public Safety.

The university maintains a heavy security presence, and access to many campus buildings requires a valid Columbia ID. The neighborhood offers incredible access to green spaces, bordered by Riverside Park to the west (overlooking the Hudson River) and Morningside Park to the east.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Columbia University an open campus?

Yes, the outdoor plazas and walkways of the Morningside Heights and Manhattanville campuses are generally open to the public during daylight hours. However, access to academic buildings, libraries, and dormitories requires a student or staff ID card.

How far apart are the Morningside and Manhattanville campuses?

They are very close. It is approximately a 10 to 15-minute walk (about half a mile) between the main gates of Morningside Heights and the heart of the Manhattanville campus.

Does Columbia guarantee housing in NYC?

Yes, Columbia College and Columbia Engineering guarantee housing for undergraduate students for all four years, provided they remain continuously enrolled. Most undergraduates live on or immediately adjacent to the Morningside Heights campus.

Frequently Asked Questions About Columbia's Location

Is Morningside Heights a safe neighborhood for students?

Yes, Morningside Heights is generally considered a very safe neighborhood in Manhattan. It is heavily populated by students, faculty, and young professionals. Columbia University also employs a robust Public Safety department that patrols the campus and surrounding blocks 24/7, and provides evening shuttle services.

Do Columbia students have time to explore New York City?

While the Core Curriculum and academic workload are notoriously demanding, most Columbia students actively engage with the city. The university offers discounted tickets to Broadway shows, museums (like the Met and MoMA), and cultural events, actively encouraging students to use NYC as an extension of the classroom.

Are freshmen required to live on campus?

Yes, all first-year undergraduate students at Columbia College and the School of Engineering are required to live on campus. Almost all upperclassmen also choose to remain in university-owned housing for all four years because securing a private apartment in Manhattan is extraordinarily expensive and complex.
